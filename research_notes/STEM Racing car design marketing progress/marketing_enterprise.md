## 1. Judging structure: judged elements, point weights, awards, and differences by class and level

### Takeaway
At the 2025 Aramco STEM Racing World Finals, 1,000 points were split as follows:
- **Enterprise judging: 180.** This is made up of the Enterprise Portfolio (100), Team Identity (20) and the Pit Display (60).
- **Verbal Presentation: 140.**
- **Project Management: 90.**

Non-engineering, non-racing judging is therefore about 41% of the total. Racing is 25% and engineering-judged elements are 34%. The UK uses a class ladder (Entry → Development → Professional, plus a Primary guidebook from 2026-27). Lower classes have shorter portfolios, and the UK names a "Brand Identity" judging area. At WF level the non-engineering work also wins stand-alone awards backed by F1 teams, such as the Sponsorship & Marketing Award supported by MoneyGram Haas F1 Team.

### Inferences
- **WF 2025 weightings:**
  - Enterprise block 180 (18%), Verbal 140 (14%) and PM 90 (9%) give 410/1,000 (41%) for "non-engineering" judging.
  - Racing is 250 (25%). Specification, drawings, renders, finish and the D&E portfolio together are 340 (34%).
  - The Enterprise block (180) carries **the same weight as the D&E Portfolio (180)**. The Verbal Presentation (which covers the whole project) is the fourth-largest block, after racing and the two 180-point blocks.
- Team Identity is only 20 points directly. However, identity also drives the Pit Display (60) and "Document Presentation" within the Enterprise Portfolio (100), so a coherent brand plausibly influences about 80–180 points.
- The named WF awards for non-engineering work (Sponsorship & Marketing, Best Application of Project Management, Pit Display, Verbal Presentation; Digital Media at NA level) mean a team can gain recognition, and sponsor-worthy publicity, even without the overall title.
- The UK "Brand Identity" label appears to be the UK name for the Enterprise/Team Identity/Pit Display cluster. Its reported 100 points probably applies to a class with a smaller total than the WF 1,000, but this is unverified.
- File timestamps in the URLs suggest the UK 2026-27 DEV/PRO regulations were posted around June 2026, and the WF 2026 technical regulations around February 2026. This is inferred from URL timestamps.

## 2. The Enterprise Portfolio: required contents, page limits, official criteria, and what separates top scores

### Takeaway
At WF level the Enterprise Portfolio is a 12-page A3 document (front cover + 10 content pages + back cover). Teams submit 1 digital and 2 physical copies. It is scored out of 100 on five criteria: **Marketing Strategy & Materials; Sponsorship & Return on Investment; Digital Media Proficiency; Sustainability; Document Presentation**. In the UK, Professional teams get 10 pages maximum and Development teams 5. Top teams back each claim with quantified evidence, and they must defend the "marketing concept and business plan" in a face-to-face judging interview.

### Inferences
- If the five WF criteria were equally weighted, each would be worth about 20 of the 100 points. This is **unverified**; the regulations' scorecard was not readable.
- The WF allows 10 content pages, the same as the UK Professional limit, so UK Professional teams are practising at the WF format. Development teams have to compress the same story into 5 pages.
- Common features of top-scoring teams, drawn from the criteria, the Sponsorship & Marketing award wording and the team accounts above:
  - A marketing strategy that runs explicitly from objectives to target audiences, tactics and measured results.
  - Sponsorship presented as two-way value, with ROI or reach delivered to each sponsor, not just a logo wall.
  - Digital-media metrics such as reach, views, interactions and non-follower share.
  - Sustainability across environmental, economic and social dimensions.
  - Highly visual, on-brand page design.
  - The ability to defend the numbers and the business plan when interviewed.
- Finance appears to straddle the Enterprise Portfolio (sponsorship and ROI, business plan) and the PM Portfolio (budget and resource management); see Section 8.

## 3. Brand identity: names, logos, colours, style guides, uniforms, merchandise, and consistency across car, pit and online

### Takeaway
Team identity is scored directly at the WF (Team Identity 20/1,000, judged as "Overall Team Identity"). In the UK it is a headline judging area, "Brand Identity" (reported at 100 points). Strong teams pick one theme and carry it through everything: sponsor tier names, uniforms, merchandise, pit display, website and portfolio. Many teams also build websites carrying sponsors, portfolios, press and sustainability pages.

### Cited Findings
- WF 2025 has a separate **Team Identity (20 points)** score, judged as "Overall Team Identity" alongside the Pit Display, with a combined "Pit Display & Team Identity Scorecard" — [SRWF 2025 Regulations (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/878610006/STEM-Racing-World-Finals-Competition-Regulations-2025) [Medium]
- In the UK the judged area is called **Brand Identity**: students "create show-stopping pit displays" — [STEM Racing UK National Finals 2026](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/stem-racing-uk-national-finals-2026) [Medium]. It is reported as worth 100 points — [UK Regulations 2025-26](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_competition_regulations_2025-2026_dev_pro.pdf) [Low-Medium]

## 4. Sponsorship: finding sponsors, tiers and packages, sponsor benefits, in-kind support, relationship management, and amounts raised

### Takeaway
Most teams sell tiered packages linked to visibility "assets": social posts, logo on uniform, logo on car, pit display, presentation mentions. At national level, tier prices seen range from about €150 to €700+ (Caravel: Sailor €0–150, Helmsman €150–400, Master €400–700, Captain €700+) and €200 Bronze and €500 Silver (Orbit Racing, Ireland). Teams seek in-kind technical partners such as CNC machining, 3D printing, logistics and university workshops. WF-bound teams must raise tens of thousands of euros. Judges and the WF award reward "mutually beneficial relationships" and ROI, not just money raised.

## 6. The pit display: requirements, dimensions, contents, judging, costs and suppliers

### Takeaway
At WF 2025 the pit display is worth 60 points, alongside Team Identity at 20. It is judged on: (1) the **design process, documented in the Enterprise Portfolio**; (2) **content, clarity and impact**; (3) **functionality and user-experience execution**. Organisers supply a booth frame and teams dress it. The North America 2024 frame was 94 inches tall; the UK template is a 3-panel booth with a 300 mm header. The enterprise judging conversation happens at the pit display, and A3 portfolio copies are exhibited there. Exact WF 2025/2026 booth dimensions could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- WF 2025 Pit Display criteria are "Design Process (Documented in Enterprise Portfolio)", "Content, Clarity, and Impact", and "Functionality & User Experience execution". The regulations include a "PIT DISPLAY REFERENCE DIMENSIONS" section; the numbers were not visible in extracts — [SRWF 2025 Regulations (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/878610006/STEM-Racing-World-Finals-Competition-Regulations-2025); [SRWF 2025 Regulations (stemracing.com)](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf) [Medium]

## 7. The verbal presentation: length, format, presenters, and how it is judged

### Takeaway
At WF level the verbal presentation lasts **10 minutes**. Teams presenting through an interpreter get 20 minutes but must notify organisers. It is worth **140/1,000 points** at WF 2025, more than the Enterprise Portfolio alone (100). Teams bring their own laptop and slides (PowerPoint or Canva). National and regional sessions are shorter. Champion teams rehearse extensively in front of outside audiences.

### Cited Findings
- "At World Finals Level teams are required to prepare a 10-minute presentation." If teams cannot present in English, an interpreter may attend and 20 minutes is allocated, provided the team notifies organisers. Teams "should bring their own laptop with any slide show or other multimedia files" and usually use PowerPoint or Canva — [SRWF 2025 Regulations (stemracing.com)](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf); [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/878610006/STEM-Racing-World-Finals-Competition-Regulations-2025) [Medium]
- WF 2025 Verbal Presentation Judging: 140 points combined — [SRWF 2025 Regulations mirror](https://static.portaldaindustria.com.br/media/filer_public/8c/fd/8cfdb641-28a4-41a1-8cd5-2a46f6c4c052/regulamento_da_competicao.pdf) [Medium]
- UK National Finals: teams "speak to industry professionals and present their work in short sessions" — [STEM Racing UK National Finals 2026](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/stem-racing-uk-national-finals-2026) [Medium]. A UK extract indicates a ten-minute verbal presentation for the Professional class; the Development class length was not found — [UK Regulations 2025-26](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_competition_regulations_2025-2026_dev_pro.pdf) [Low-Medium]
- UK Entry class: the verbal presentation involves speaking directly to the judges, optionally with a PowerPoint — [UK Entry Guidebook 2025-26](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026.pdf) [Medium]

  - It won "Best verbal Presentation" at WF 2023.

### Inferences
- At 140 points, a strong 10-minute presentation is worth about 1.4 times the Enterprise Portfolio. It therefore merits a rehearsal programme like Recoil's, with external audiences, timing drills and scripted role hand-offs.
- Because the presentation covers "all aspects of the challenge", it probably needs to integrate engineering, project management and enterprise stories in one narrative, with enterprise results (sponsor ROI, reach) as proof points.

### Gaps
- The judging rubric and sub-weights within the 140 points (content versus delivery versus Q&A) were not found.
- No rule was found on the number of presenters, whether all members must speak, or the length of a Q&A.
- The UK Development-class duration and Australian national and state durations are unconfirmed.

## 8. Fundraising and finance: season budgets, cost items, tracking income and spending, and how finance appears in portfolios

### Takeaway
Costs scale sharply by level. A Portuguese national-level team reported about **€2,156 total spend, including a €1,555 marketing budget**. Irish teams qualifying for the WF 2026 in Singapore estimated **€40,000–€50,000** for the trip, with public fundraising targets of €25,000–€50,000. Funding mixes tiered business sponsorship, in-kind technical partners, community fundraising (bake sales, merchandise, GoFundMe) and school or education-board support. Strong teams show cash-flow forecasts and receipted expense logs (PM portfolio) and sponsorship ROI (Enterprise portfolio).

### Inferences
- **Order-of-magnitude budget ladder** (low confidence; based on a handful of teams):
  - Regional to national, domestic: low thousands of euros or pounds. Marketing, merchandise and the pit display can be a large share. If Caravel's €1,555 marketing budget is a subset of its €2,156 actual spend (the source does not say), marketing was about 72% of spend.
  - WF qualification: roughly €40–50k per team. Travel and accommodation for students plus supervising adults dominate.
  - The WF jump explains why winning teams formalise tiered partner programmes and pursue press coverage after qualifying.
- **Evidence judges probably expect** on finance:
  - A budget and cash-flow forecast.
  - An income-versus-expenditure record with receipts.
  - Sponsorship income by tier or partner, including an in-kind valuation.
  - A link from spending decisions to objectives, such as reusing pit materials.
  - ROI reporting back to sponsors.

### Gaps
- UK (and other national) registration or entry fees for 2025-26 and 2026-27 were not found.
- No official guidance was found on whether finance belongs in the Enterprise or the PM portfolio, and no finance-specific scoring criteria.
- No systematic survey of team budgets exists. The figures above are individual team disclosures, and currencies and years vary.
- In-kind sponsorship valuations (for example, the value of CNC machining time donated) were not reported by any team found.
