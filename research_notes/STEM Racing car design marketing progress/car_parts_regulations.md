## 1. What physically makes up a competition car (bill of materials by class)?

### Takeaway
A STEM Racing car has these parts: a body machined from the official foam Model Block, which comes with an 18 mm pre-drilled hole for the 8 g CO2 "power pack"; four wheels on two axles (bushes or bearings); two tether line guides; a front wing (a nose-cone/front-wing assembly in the Development class) and a rear wing; a mandatory standard **halo**, which is also the car's braking interface after the finish line; a mandatory standard downloadable **helmet**; a "virtual cargo" volume inside the body; and A/B identification decals. Entry and Development cars are built mainly from a supplied starter kit; Development teams may 3D-print their own wheels. Professional/World Finals teams design most parts themselves, within dimensional limits.

#### Body / official model block
- The Denford "STEM Racing Model Block Blank" measures **223 mm × 65 mm × 50 mm**, has a **consistent weight of 114 g**, and has an **18 mm pre-drilled hole for the Power Pack**. It is sold singly and in packs of 10. — [Denford: Model Block Blank (Single)](https://denford.co.uk/shop/stem-racing-model-block-blank-single); [Denford: Model Block Blank (Pack of 10)](https://denford.co.uk/shop/stem-racing-model-block-blank-pack-of-10)
- Rule wording from the F1 in Schools era defines the official Model Block as "a rigid, closed cell foam block processed to the dimensional features as shown by diagrams in Appendix ii" of the technical regulations. The exact document and year were not pinned; the extract drew on the F1 in Schools regs and Denford's 2015 data sheet. — [F1 in Schools N.A. Dev Class Tech Regs 2024-25](https://f1inschoolsna.squarespace.com/s/F1iS-DEVCLASS_NATechnicalRegulations2024-25v2.pdf); [Denford F1 Model Block Technical Data Sheet (29.6.15)](https://denfordata.com/downloads/F1%20Model%20Block%20Technical%20Data%20Sheet%2029.6.15.pdf)
- Denford also sells a "STEM Racing Model Block Car Kit", a "Model Block Blank – shipped to UCL MechSpace for manufacturing" (a service where the block is sent to a university workshop to be machined), and a "STEM Racing 4th Axis Car Manufacturing Conversion Kit" for CNC machining. — [Denford: Model Block Car Kit](https://denford.co.uk/shop/stem-racing-model-block-car-kit); [Denford: shipped to UCL MechSpace](https://denford.co.uk/shop/stem-racing-model-block-blank-shipped-to-ucl-mechspace-for-manufacturing); [Denford: 4th Axis conversion kit](https://denford.co.uk/shop/stem-racing-4th-axis-car-manufacturing-conversion-kit)

#### Power unit (CO2 cartridge) and cartridge chamber
- Development cars "are powered only by a single compressed air cartridge containing 8 grams of pressurised CO2". — [UK Development Technical Regulations 2025-26](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)
- Definition: the "power unit cartridge chamber is a cylindrical clear space bounded around its inner circumference and forward end by car body only". Older wording says there must be no obstruction to the chamber when viewed from the rear, and it must be "free and clear of any objects". — reported from [WF2026 Tech Regs](https://www.stemracing.com/s/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations-1.pdf) / [UK Pro 2025-26 (WF2025 V1)](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf); older wording from [F1 in Schools WF2022 Tech Regs](https://static.portaldaindustria.com.br/media/filer_public/8d/ef/8def9a22-c670-4909-9b30-8ace1cc3714a/regulamento_tecnico_2022___ingles.pdf)
- Reported chamber limits (clause numbers not returned; document not pinned between the UK Pro 2025-26/WF2025, WF2026, UK Dev and Poland 2025-26 PDFs):
  - diameter **18.0 mm minimum, 18.5 mm maximum**, measured at any point through the minimum depth;
  - depth **45 mm minimum, 58 mm maximum**;
  - distance from track surface ("8g Pack") **30 mm minimum, 40 mm maximum**;
  - maximum chamber angle ("for 8g pack") **−3° to +3°**;
  - chamber "safety zone" ("for 8g pack") **3 mm minimum**.
  — [UK Pro 2025-26 / WF2025 V1](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf); [WF2026 Tech Regs](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/68b858da4556365ec586dae9/t/698b53e09fc8321849034226/1770738656680/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations+1.pdf); [Poland Tech Regs 2025/26](https://stemracing.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/STEM-Racing-Poland-Technical-Regulations-2025-26.pdf)
- Some regulations are checked with a full **8.0 g race cartridge fully inserted**. In that state the car "must be capable of resting equally on all four (4) wheels without any outside assistance". — same documents (not pinned)

#### Wheels
- A car must have **four wheels**, and all four must touch the track surface at all times. A wheel is "a single part, cylindrical in form, with its maximum circumference contacting the track surface". All material within the volume of its extreme diameter and width counts as part of the wheel. The rule sections are titled "T7.4 Track contact width" and "T7.5 Diameter" in the North American 2024-25 regs. — [STEM Racing USA & Canada Tech Regs 2024-25](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5d82b3ff7c2dda76efc9a139/t/67721b19a934c9553b25fb7e/1735531297022/F1iS-NATechnicalRegulations2024-25v2.pdf)
- Entry class: cars **must use the STEM Racing standard wheel, unmodified**, which comes in the Entry starter kit. — [UK Entry Class Guidebook 2025-26](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026.pdf)
- Development class: every car is made with the starter kit "except for wheels, which can be 3D printed". — [UK Dev Tech Regs 2025-26](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)
- Professional/WF: track-contact width and diameter are PERFORMANCE regulations with a penalty of **2.5 points per millimetre per wheel** (reported from pages 31–32). — [UK Pro 2025-26 (WF2025 V1)](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf); [F1 in Schools WF2024 Tech Regs](https://www.stemracing.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/f1swf_2024_technical_regulations.pdf)
- **2022 (older):** the minimum wheel diameter was raised to **28 mm** for the 2022 F1 in Schools World Finals. — [Off The Track (2021)](https://offthetrack.net/news/wheel-diameter-set-to-increase-at-2022-f1-in-schools-world-finals-07-2021/)
- WF2026 added an optional **Pit Stop Challenge** on the wheel–axle interface, "to reduce wheel breakage" and make wheels quick to replace. The mounting must be reusable and allow a non-destructive change "without the use of adhesives or permanent fixings" (for example pins, fasteners or positive interlocking features), and must stay compliant with Article 7. A single wheel change must take **under 60 seconds**, checked in live scrutineering. There is a Pit Stop Award. — [STEM Racing: WF2026 "Understanding the Technical Regulations"](https://www.stemracing.com/news/aramco-stem-racing-world-finals-2026-understanding-the-technical-regulations)
- Australia (REA) publishes an "Official REA Standard Wheel Kit" and its specification inside its 2025 Dev/Pro technical regulations. — [REA 2025 Australian Technical Regulations v2.0 (Dev & Pro)](https://rea.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025-Australian-Technical-Regulations-v2.0-Dev-Pro-Classes.pdf)

#### Axles, bushes, bearings
- Entry class: cars must use **two standard axles and axle bushes**, "modified only in length within specified dimensions" (kit parts). — [UK Entry Guidebook 2025-26](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026.pdf)
- The UK starter kit has **4 wheels, 4 axle bushes, 2 axles and 2 tether line guides**, described as "the minimum needed to enter". — [UK Entry Guidebook 2025-26](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026.pdf); [STEM Racing UK rules page](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/rules-regulations)
- Denford project kits contain wheels, axles, axle bushes and "axle guides". — [Denford webshop: Primary STEM Project Pack](https://denfordwebshop.com/index.php?route=product/product&product_id=444)
- Bearing practice (vendor source): **silicon nitride ceramic bearings** for minimal rolling resistance. A team wheel design used a **7.2 mm wheel bore for 7 mm outside-diameter bearings**. The extract also said the bore was "increased by 0.5 mm", which does not match 7.2 mm; treat that detail as unclear. The vendor claims cars reach "up to 95 km/h". — [Boca Bearings: F1 in Schools bearing and wheel design (2018)](https://www.bocabearings.com/blogs/2018/f1-in-schools-bearing-and-wheel-design); [Boca Bearings: how to prepare for F1 in Schools](https://www.bocabearings.com/f1-school-bearing-tips-tutorials/how-to-prepare-for-a-f1-in-schools-competition)

#### Tether line guides
- A tether line guide is "a key safety component which completely surrounds the track tether line so as to safely connect the car to the tether line during races". Guides "must be robust so as to prevent the diameter or shape changing during racing". — reported from [UK Dev Tech Regs 2025-26](https://www.scribd.com/document/967290440/STEM-Racing-Development-Technical-Regulations-2025-2026) / [WF2026 Tech Regs](https://www.stemracing.com/s/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations-1.pdf) (document not pinned)
- Scrutineering test: a **200 g weight is hung from each tether line guide** to check it is securely fitted. — same (document not pinned; also in the result set: [REA 2025 regs](https://www.stemwa.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-Australian-Technical-Regulations-v2.0-Dev-Pro-Classes.pdf))
- Entry class: cars must use **two standard tether line guide screw eyes** from the Entry starter kit, "secured safely into the underside of the car body". — [UK Entry Guidebook 2025-26](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026.pdf)
- **Older figures:** internal diameter is measured with a **3.5 mm and/or 6.0 mm diameter tool**, with a **minimum of 3.5 mm** ([WF2022 Tech Regs](https://static.portaldaindustria.com.br/media/filer_public/8d/ef/8def9a22-c670-4909-9b30-8ace1cc3714a/regulamento_tecnico_2022___ingles.pdf); [F1 in Schools UK Pro Tech Regs 2021-22](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/f1_in_schools_technical_regulations_2021-2022_professional.pdf)). US Developmental Class **2019/20**: minimum 3 mm, maximum 5 mm ([F1iS US Dev Class Tech Regs 19-20](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5d82b3ff7c2dda76efc9a139/t/5d9f4641b8513d4f555ae3dc/1570719306361/F1iS-USDEVCLASSTechnicalRegulations19-20.pdf)).

#### Wings and nose cone
- Reported for the current documents (class not pinned; result set was REA 2025 Dev/Pro, WF2026, UK Dev 2025-26 and 2026-27, UK Entry 2025-26): front wing **minimum span 50 mm**, **thickness 2–6 mm**, and **≥ 5 mm clear air space** to any other part of the car or the track. Rear wing: **a single, unbroken minimum span of 50 mm**, thickness 2–6 mm, ≥ 5 mm clear air space. — [REA 2025 Tech Regs](https://rea.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025-Australian-Technical-Regulations-v2.0-Dev-Pro-Classes.pdf); [WF2026 Tech Regs](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/68b858da4556365ec586dae9/t/698b53e09fc8321849034226/1770738656680/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations+1.pdf); [UK Dev 2026-27](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d66b2c057810a68895f00/1780311730822/STEM+Racing+Development+Technical+Regulations+2026-2027.pdf)
- Wing thickness is defined as "the deepest point of the wing across its cross section". — [F1 in Schools N.A. Dev Tech Regs 2024-25](https://f1inschoolsna.squarespace.com/s/F1iS-DEVCLASS_NATechnicalRegulations2024-25v2.pdf)
- The Development spares list allows **up to 2 "nose cone & front wing assemblies"** and **2 rear wing assemblies**, so nose and front wing form one assembly in that class. — [UK Dev Tech Regs 2025-26](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)

#### Halo (also the deceleration interface)
- The halo is being introduced "not only to echo real Formula 1® car design but also as a component of a new car deceleration system". It "MUST be included in the final car design" and "must be well adhered to the body". That needs "a recess or pocket to match the underside of the Halo", designed so it "can be manufactured using standard machining tools". — [WF2026 Tech Regs](https://www.stemracing.com/s/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations-1.pdf); [WF2025 V1 (Brazil mirror)](https://stemracingbrazil.com.br/files/Regulamentos%20T%C3%A9cnicos%20Classe%20Profissional%20para%20Final%20Mundial%202025.pdf)
- "The halo platform and no-go-zone have been developed to ensure the centre of the Halo 'Circular notch' for the deceleration system is exactly **34.0 mm ±1.0 mm above the track surface**." Fitting the halo needs "**two 6 mm holes to be cut 40 mm apart** (using the jig provided)" per Appendix iv. After the finish line, an **arresting cable** in the final track section engages the notch and slows the car. — reported from [UK Dev Tech Regs 2025-26](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf) / [UK Entry Guidebook 2025-26](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/692ec6999b44cc201847e7c6/1764673177837/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026+(1).pdf)
- The Entry class states the same 34.0 mm (±1.0 mm) notch-centre height. — [UK Entry Guidebook 2025-26](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026.pdf)
- Race2Learn (US) sells a "STEM Racing Race System Package – Elevated with Halo Deceleration System". — [Race2Learn](https://race2learn.net/products/f1-race-system-package-elevated-with-halo-deceleration-system)

#### Driver helmet
- "The Helmet is a standard part designed by STEM Racing that MUST be included in the car design without any dimensional changes." It can be downloaded "as a universal 3D part from the STEM Racing website" and "may be manufactured out of any material". — [UK Dev Tech Regs 2025-26](https://www.scribd.com/document/967290440/STEM-Racing-Development-Technical-Regulations-2025-2026); [WF2026 Tech Regs](https://www.stemracing.com/s/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations-1.pdf); [STEM Racing downloads](https://www.stemracing.com/downloads)

#### Virtual cargo
- The virtual cargo "must be completely encompassed by the body and be wholly positioned between the front and rear wheel centre lines", and must sit horizontal to the track surface. Its location and compliance "MUST be clearly dimensioned (such as from either the front or rear axle centre lines and height above the track)". It must be marked "by hatching, shading or block colour, or outlined with a contrasting colour line" in the engineering drawings submitted for scrutineering. — [WF2026 Tech Regs](https://www.stemracing.com/s/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations-1.pdf); [WF2025 Rev 2 (studylib mirror)](https://studylib.net/doc/27910678/stem-racing-world-finals-2025-technical-regulations---rev...)

#### Identification / decals
- Each team makes two identical race cars and fixes decals marking which is raced (**A**) and which is the replacement (**B**) if there is a breakage. — [UK Competition Regulations 2025-26 (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/955986098/Stem-Racing-Competition-Regulations-2025-2026-Dev-Pro); [WF Competition Regulations 2025](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)

- Allowed spares include **up to 2 nose cone & front wing assemblies, 2 rear wing assemblies, 4 front wheels**, "and other structural components". ### Inferences
- In Entry and Development the chamber diameter is effectively set by the block's 18 mm pre-drilled hole. That fits the Pro/WF window of 18.0–18.5 mm: teams can use the hole as supplied, or ream it by at most 0.5 mm.
- The halo is now a **functional safety part**: its notch catches the arresting cable at a fixed 34 mm height. So body height and the halo platform around it are tightly constrained. The "(for 8g pack)" labels suggest the World Finals rules are written to allow other power-unit sizes in future (speculative).
- In the Development class, nose cone and front wing count as one replaceable assembly, which suggests the kit or rules treat them as one part.

### Gaps
- No current clause numbers or numbers found for: Entry-class dimensions and minimum weight; Pro/WF total length; wheelbase; track width; ground clearance; wheel diameter and width limits for 2025-26 or 2026-27 (only the older 2022 minimum of 28 mm, and the 2.5 pts/mm penalty, were found); wing chord; nose-cone position; virtual-cargo size; helmet size; halo size beyond the notch height and fixing holes; sidepod rules; no-go-zone geometry; decal, paint and surface-finish rules; team-number or logo rules; ballast, adhesive and fastener rules.
- Whether Pro/WF 2026 still requires the official model block for the body was not confirmed.
- Tether-guide spacing/positions and the current maximum internal diameter were not found.
- Bearing size is unverified. The often-quoted 3 × 7 × 3 mm (bore × outside diameter × width) is only consistent with the 7 mm outside diameter in the Boca blog. No source confirmed the bore or width.

## 2. Key technical regulation figures by class, season and country

### Takeaway
There are three secondary classes: Entry (kit car), Development (kit car, own 3D-printed wheels allowed, minimum 60.0 g) and Professional/World Finals (team-designed, minimum 50.0 g, width 65–85 mm, height ≤ 65 mm). Development length is 170–210 mm. Rules are grouped as GENERAL, SAFETY or PERFORMANCE. The WF2026 rules are refinements, not a rewrite: the headline change is an optional wheel-change "Pit Stop Challenge". National operators (UK, USA/Canada, Australia/REA, Poland, Ireland, UAE, Malaysia and others) publish their own versions. The UK Professional class for 2025-26 simply uses the World Finals 2025 technical regulations.

#### Rule structure and class differences
- Technical rules fall into three categories: **GENERAL** ("shaping the car's fundamental look"), **SAFETY** ("mandatory rules for safe racing") and **PERFORMANCE** (rules that directly affect vehicle performance). — [UK Dev Tech Regs 2025-26](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)
- Development article numbers carry a **"D" prefix** in 2026-27, and the document "is mainly concerned with those regulations that are directly related to STEM Racing car design and manufacture". — [UK Dev Tech Regs 2026-27](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d66b2c057810a68895f00/1780311730822/STEM+Racing+Development+Technical+Regulations+2026-2027.pdf)

## 3. Scrutineering: measurement, penalty system, car A/B, and common infringements

### Takeaway
Specification judging starts each team at 100 points (110 in some competitions). Both race cars are checked in a closed Parc Fermé using purpose-made gauges, and scales for weight. Each infringement is deducted once per car. Dimensional and weight misses are penalised in proportion, per started 1 mm or 1 g ("PP+"). Examples: 2.5 pts per mm per wheel for wheel diameter or contact width (Pro); 10 pts per started gram underweight (Development, up to 30 pts). Tether guides are load-tested with 200 g. No published list of the "most common" infringements was found.

### Cited Findings
- Teams begin specification judging with **100 points** ("some competitions use 110 points"). **Both race cars** are assessed, and deductions apply to infringements on either car, "only applied once, per infringement, per car". — [UK Competition Regs 2025-26](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6936b74056d414042f3a6e33/1765193536812/stem_racing_competition_regulations_2025-2026_dev_pro+(1).pdf); [WF Competition Regs 2025](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf); [Ireland Competition Regs 2025-26](https://www.stemracing.ie/_files/ugd/a8be99_bd55cc622a09434b883c1911af10b22d.pdf?index=true)
- **Proportional penalties ("PP+" symbol):** the penalty grows with the margin of non-compliance. The miss is rounded up to the next complete unit (1.0 mm or 1.0 g), and the penalty is applied once per complete unit outside the absolute min/max. — same sources
- Specification and scrutineering judging is **closed**; no team member or supervising teacher may attend. It takes place in **Parc Fermé**, where judges check all cars and any optional replacement components. "A series of specially manufactured gauges will be used to broadly check compliance." — same sources
- Penalty values seen: Development weight **10 pts for 0.1–1.0 g under, up to 30 pts for 2.1–3.0 g under** ([UK Dev 2025-26](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)). Pro wheel diameter and track-contact width **2.5 pts per mm per wheel** ([UK Pro 2025-26](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf)).
- Tether guide safety test: a 200 g weight hung from each guide. — [UK Dev Tech Regs 2025-26](https://www.scribd.com/document/967290440/STEM-Racing-Development-Technical-Regulations-2025-2026) (document not pinned)
- Some regulations are checked with a full 8.0 g cartridge inserted; the car must rest on all four wheels unaided. — [UK Pro 2025-26 (WF2025 V1)](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf) (document not pinned)
- Drawing-based compliance: the virtual cargo's position must be dimensioned and hatched in the engineering drawings "submitted for scrutineering". — [WF2026 Tech Regs](https://www.stemracing.com/s/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations-1.pdf)
- **Car A / Car B:** each team must produce **two identical race cars**, marked with decals as A (the car that races) and B (the replacement after a breakage). — [UK Competition Regs 2025-26 (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/955986098/Stem-Racing-Competition-Regulations-2025-2026-Dev-Pro); [WF Competition Regs 2025](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)
- WF2026 Pit Stop Challenge: the wheel change is verified "during live scrutineering". Judges assess robustness, clarity of the mechanism and replacement time (under 60 s). — [STEM Racing WF2026 article](https://www.stemracing.com/news/aramco-stem-racing-world-finals-2026-understanding-the-technical-regulations)
- The 2026 review explicitly targets "common compliance issues" and "areas that previously caused confusion during scrutineering". It advises teams to check compliance "during CAD, before manufacturing, and again before submission", and to treat scrutineering "as part of the engineering process, not an afterthought". — same article
- **Older (2017 Pro; 2018-19 South Africa):** a car judged non-compliant with any **critical regulation** is **ineligible for National Champions, Fastest Car and Best Engineered Car**. — [F1 in Schools Pro Class Tech Regs 2017](https://sangari.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/F1-Professional-Class-technical-regulations-2017.pdf); [MAHLE F1 in Schools How-to-Build Regs 2018-19](https://mahle-f1schoolschallenge.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/MAHLE_F1-in-Schools-HOW-TO-BUILD-Regulations-2018-2019.pdf)
- **USA/Canada 2024-25:** late submission carries a **20-point penalty**. Scrutineering judging covers engineering drawings, rendering and quality of finish and assembly. Teams bring a printed **5-page Design & Engineering Portfolio in Tabloid (11" × 17")** format. — [NA Competition Regs 2024-25 REV1](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5d82b3ff7c2dda76efc9a139/t/665e216dcb046c3ba4229923/1717444975159/F1iS-NACompetitionRegulations2024_25_REV1.pdf); [NA Dev Tech Regs 2024-25](https://f1inschoolsna.squarespace.com/s/F1iS-DEVCLASS_NATechnicalRegulations2024-25v2.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because penalties apply per car and per wheel, one systematic dimensional error is multiplied. For example, four wheels each 1 mm under the Pro limit would cost 4 × 2.5 = 10 pts per car, and 20 pts if both A and B share the error.
- In Development, 1 g underweight costs 10 pts. Teams therefore have a strong reason to build a little above 60.0 g, since paint, decals and wear change the mass.
- WF2026 added a wheel-change challenge specifically "to reduce wheel breakage", and the review mentions "common compliance issues". Wheel/axle failures and ambiguous areas (virtual cargo documentation, halo fitting) are probably recurring problems. This is an inference, not a published ranking.

- No published ranking of the most common infringements, and no judges' guide, was found. Team write-ups describing their penalties were not found either.
- The points-per-gram penalty for the Pro/WF minimum weight, penalty values for other dimensions, and the exact scale resolution or gauge designs were not found.
- A "display car" requirement (in addition to cars A and B) was **not confirmed** for any 2025-26 or 2026-27 competition. The results mention only pit-display space.
- Re-scrutineering, damaged-car procedures and replacement-component penalties were not found.

## 5. The race: track, tether, launch, CO2, timing and typical times

### Takeaway
Cars run 20 m down a multi-lane track (4 lanes, generally 2 at World Finals), guided by a nylon tether line. Launch pods pierce the 8 g CO2 cartridges with steel firing pins and start the timers. Two infrared beams at the finish stop the clock to 0.001 s. In reaction races, the driver's reaction to five start lights is recorded separately and subtracted. Elite times are just over one second: at the 2025 World Finals, champions Lunar (Australia) ran 1.082 s and are listed with 1.118 s for Fastest Car. After the finish, a cable catches the halo notch to stop the car.

### Cited Findings
- Cars race a **20 m** track with **four lanes**, generally **2 lanes at the World Finals**. They are attached to the track by a **nylon wire** (tether), powered by CO2 cartridges, and timed from launch to finish line. — [Wikipedia: STEM Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEM_Racing)
- Cars cover "a 20-metres distance in just over a second". — [Yas Marina Circuit: F1 in Schools](https://www.yasmarinacircuit.com/en/yas-in-school/f1-in-schools)
- **Reaction time:** "the time recorded from the instant the five (5) start lights extinguish to the instant the start trigger is activated by the driver". **Car race time:** from the instant the launch pod fires until the car breaks the finish-line timing beam. In reaction races, car race time = the total race time shown on the electronic start gate minus the reaction time. — [UK Competition Regs 2026-27](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d9375a869f15bc0bbdf98/1780323189128/STEM+Racing+Competition+Regulations+2026-2027+DEV+PRO+(1).pdf); [WF Competition Regs 2025](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)
- Launch and timing hardware: pressing the launch button sends an electronic pulse that fires both launch pods at once. Their **steel firing pins punch the CO2 cartridges** and start the digital timers. At the finish gate each car breaks **two infrared beams**, which stop the timers. Times are shown to **0.001 s**, and flashing lights mark the winning lane. Modes: **Auto, Manual and Reaction Time**. — [Denford Race Package – Roll Out Track](https://denford.co.uk/products/stem-racing-race-system-package-roll-out); [Denford Race Control System](https://denford.co.uk/products/stem-racing-race-system-package-stand-alone)
- Deceleration: an **arresting cable** in the final track section catches the halo's circular notch (34.0 ± 1.0 mm above the track) after the finish line. — [UK Dev Tech Regs 2025-26](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)
- **2025 World Finals:** Team **Lunar (Australia)** were crowned 2025 World Champions and won the Fastest Car Award. The search extract gives the "fastest car time" as **1.118 s** and also says Lunar ran a **1.082 s** lap. The two figures conflict (the award may use a different or averaged metric); check against the source. — [STEM Racing: Lunar crowned 2025 World Champions](https://www.stemracing.com/news/lunar-crowned-aramco-stem-racing-world-champions-2025); [D&T Association: WF2025](https://www.designtechnology.org.uk/news/aramco-stem-racing-world-finals-2025/)
- Vendor claim: cars "reach speeds of up to 95 km/h". — [Boca Bearings blog](https://www.bocabearings.com/blogs/2018/f1-in-schools-bearing-and-wheel-design)
- Power: a single 8 g CO2 cartridge (Development). RACE-grade packs are within ±0.5 g. — [UK Dev 2025-26](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf); [Denford Power Packs](https://denford.co.uk/shop/denford-power-packs-8gm-pack-of-360)

### Inferences
- Average speed over 20 m: 1.082 s → about **18.5 m/s (66.5 km/h)**; 1.118 s → about **17.9 m/s (64.4 km/h)**. Peak speed is higher than the average because the car accelerates and then coasts. The 95 km/h vendor figure is plausible as a peak but unverified.
- Physics (standard dynamics, not from a STEM Racing source): the CO2 gives a roughly fixed impulse, so peak speed and acceleration fall as the moving mass (car plus cartridge) rises. The regulated minimum mass (50 g Pro, 60 g Development) sets the floor. Aerodynamic drag grows with the square of speed and dominates the coasting phase. Bearing and tether-guide friction act over the whole 20 m. This is why teams tune toward minimum weight, low frontal area and drag, low-friction bearings and light wheels.
- Timing is to 0.001 s, and elite cars differ by hundredths of a second. So small reductions in mass or drag can change race rankings.

### Gaps

- Tether line material, diameter and tension, the tether height, and the track surface specification were not found.
- The race format (number of runs, knockout structure, how points are allocated for racing) and typical times by class (Entry vs Development vs Pro) were not found. Only elite World Finals times of about 1.08–1.12 s were sourced.
- Whether the cartridge counts toward the regulated minimum weight, and the mass of a charged 8 g cartridge, were not found.
