# How STEM Racing (formerly F1 in Schools) teams keep car and project progress steady through a season

*Method note for the report writer: every finding below comes from WebSearch result summaries. WebFetch and direct HTTP requests were blocked by the environment's egress proxy for every domain tried (stemracing.com, stemracing.us, stemracing.ca, squarespace-hosted regulation PDFs, medium.com, wikipedia.org, pixeldrive.in, ascentracing.org and others), and the session's shared web-search budget ran out before the planned final searches. Quotes are therefore as the search tool rendered them, not checked against full text. Confidence tags: **[High]** = several consistent sources or an official source with specific detail; **[Medium]** = one credible or official source seen only through a summary; **[Low]** = unclear attribution, title only, or not STEM Racing-specific. Items marked "older" pre-date the 2023-24 season or the rename from F1 in Schools to STEM Racing.*

## 1. Season timeline: registration, heats, national finals, World Finals, submission deadlines (by country)

### Takeaway
Each season is built around the national operator's calendar and a single World Finals in the autumn. The World Finals were in Singapore in late Sep/early Oct 2025 and are set for 3–7 Oct 2026. In the UK, USA and Canada, registration opens Aug–Sep, regional heats run Nov–Feb and national finals are in Mar–Apr. Australia and Saudi Arabia hold national finals around late May–June. World Finals portfolios are due about two weeks before the event. A team that goes all the way therefore runs a campaign of roughly 12–14 months from kick-off to the World Finals.

### Inferences
- **Typical lead times** (worked out from the dates above, not stated by any source):
  - UK: school starts in September. Regionals come about 2–5 months later (Nov–Feb), nationals about 6.5 months later (late March), and the World Finals about 13 months later (the following October).
  - USA: about 10 weeks from registration opening to regional car submission. If a team registers at the 6 Nov deadline, that shrinks to under 3 weeks. Nationals come about 7 months in (mid-April) and the World Finals about 13 months in.
  - Saudi Arabia and Australia: about 4 months from national finals (late May or June) to the World Finals (late Sep/early Oct).
- A qualifying team effectively has **two seasons**: the national campaign, then a summer rebuild for the World Finals. The rebuild overlaps exams and, for final-year students, graduation. Team continuity and handover planning therefore matter.
- Because the World Finals portfolio deadline is two weeks before the event, anything changed on the car after mid-September cannot appear in the judged documents. The portfolio's design story has to be frozen before the last race-prep changes.
- Early deadlines in the USA (car in late November, documents a week later) mean US teams have far less time to their first judged event than UK teams, whose regionals may not be until February.

## 2. Team structure: size, standard roles, teacher/mentor role, how responsibilities are split and handed over

### Takeaway
Teams have 3–6 students. The standard five roles are Team Manager, Design Engineer, Manufacturing Engineer, Graphic Designer and Resource Manager. The 2026-27 Dev/Pro regulations make the Team Manager formally responsible for project management of all key deliverables and for car readiness, and the Resource Manager for budget and resource tracking. Sources say very little about the teacher's role or about handovers.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- With five named roles and as few as three students, small teams must combine roles. Because the regulations give project management to the Team Manager, that person is in practice the project manager and owns the PM portfolio.
- The Resource Manager's formal remit (tracking expenses and income, allocating funds) makes the budget ledger a natural source of dated evidence for the PM and Enterprise portfolios.
- A RACI matrix (as in the Brazilian team's tool) is how teams record who does what once one role spans several people or one person holds several roles.

## 3. Planning methods and artefacts teams use and judges expect

### Takeaway
The organiser's Project Management Guide (2024/25), produced with the PMI Educational Foundation (PMIEF), follows PMBOK and PMIEF's "Project Management Skills for Life". It covers planning, scheduling (Gantt), risk, stakeholders, budgeting and continuous improvement. Tools built by teams show RACI matrices, risk registers and resource/budget logs in real use. A widely read student critique (around 2020) argues that Gantt charts are often wasted effort and prefers a work breakdown structure plus micro-task Kanban lists.

### Cited Findings
- The STEM Racing Project Management Guide 2024/25 was produced with PMIEF. Its guidance is aligned with PMBOK® (8th Edition) principles and PMIEF's Project Management Skills for Life® framework. It gives "practical guidance on planning, scheduling, risk management, stakeholder engagement, budgeting, and continuous improvement". **[Medium–High]** — [STEM Racing PM Guide 2024/25 (PDF)](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_-_project_management_guide_2025.pdf); [STEM Racing Global: PMIEF partner page](https://www.stemracing.com/partners/pmief); [Brazil-hosted copy (title reads 2023/24)](https://stemracingbrazil.com.br/files/stem_racing_project_management_guide_2025.pdf)
- The Guide defines a Gantt chart as "a bar chart that shows a schedule, with activities listed on the vertical axis, dates on the horizontal axis, and the time it takes to do each activity shown as horizontal bars". It suggests tables in Word, charts in Excel, or PM software: Asana, JIRA, Microsoft Project, Miro, Monday, Smartsheet, Trello or projectmanager.com. **[Medium]** — [STEM Racing PM Guide 2024/25](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_-_project_management_guide_2025.pdf)
- The judging scorecards give "key performance indicators which judges use when awarding points". The handbook is meant to help teams "make informed decisions, track progress effectively, and build strong evidence for Project Management judging". **[Medium]** — [STEM Racing Global: PMIEF](https://www.stemracing.com/partners/pmief)
- The 2026-27 Dev/Pro regulations say that, with PMIEF, STEM Racing "has created a supplementary resource to help equip teams with industry-level Project Management terminology". Rule changes are marked "NEW!". **[Medium]** — [Competition Regulations 2026-27 Dev/Pro](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d9375a869f15bc0bbdf98/1780323189128/STEM+Racing+Competition+Regulations+2026-2027+DEV+PRO+(1).pdf)
- PMIEF has worked with F1 in Schools since 2020 to build project management principles into the programme. Students apply the concepts in their projects and show them at competitions. **[Medium]** — [ProjectManagement.com: How Formula 1 & PMIEF are speeding up STEM education](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/916135/how-formula-1---pmief-are-speeding-up-stem-education-)
- The Brazilian team-built tool has a "Risk Management" route in which "users identify the risks of the competition and analyze them in various ways". It also has a RACI matrix and pages to "register resources to be acquired, plan their acquisitions, and record financial movements". **[Medium]** — [GitHub: PedroLutz/stemmanagement](https://github.com/PedroLutz/stemmanagement)
- Work breakdown structure as described by a student practitioner (older, around 2020–21): deliverables are broken down into "phases, high-level deliverables and work packages" in a chronological and hierarchical structure, with a goal or mission at the top. Example phases are forming a team, creating, displaying, and moving work to the event location. **[Medium]** — [Pannya Trehan, LinkedIn: How to use the work breakdown structure in F1 in Schools](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-use-work-breakdown-structure-f1-schools-pannya-trehan)
- Counterpoint on Gantt charts (older, around 2020). Trehan argues that "the large majority of planning teams undertake is both ineffective and an active waste of time" and that "many students dive straight into using a gantt chart without knowing what each phase/step of the competition entails". He recommends "breaking large tasks & projects into micro-steps & highly detailed to-do lists, which are then organised by urgency, size, importance & relevance", run on a Kanban-style system, with Pomodoro blocks for focus. **[Medium]** — [Pannya Trehan, Medium: Why you will never have enough time in F1 in Schools and Gantt charts won't help](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/why-you-will-never-have-enough-time-in-f1-in-schools-and-gantt-charts-wont-help-65b58800d24d); [Podcast episode: why Gantt charts are terrible](https://anchor.fm/f1inschoolsstudentlife/episodes/Time-management-in-the-F1-in-Schools-competition---why-Gantt-charts-are-terrible-eoff6n)
- Off The Track (a STEM Racing news and tutorials site) calls project management "a critical pillar to the success of any team" and "a requirement of the competition at the World Finals and in many countries". Its podcast features David Palfreeman, a professional project manager who has judged World Finals in Singapore and Abu Dhabi and national finals in Australia, explaining how teams should run PM and document it for maximum points. **[Medium]** — [Off The Track podcast: How to do Project Management in F1 in Schools](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/offthetrack/episodes/How-to-do-Project-Management-in-F1-in-Schools-e2efbo4); [Palfreeman Services: podcasts & publications](https://palfreeman.com/podcasts-and-publications/)

## 4. Weekly routines: meeting frequency, lunchtime/after-school sessions, holiday sprints, tracking who does what, balancing exams

### Takeaway
There is little recent first-hand data. Older UK guidance sets a minimum of about one hour a week (an "F1 Club") over two half-terms before regionals. The most detailed first-hand critique (Trehan, 2020) says long whole-team meetings are low-value "shallow work". It recommends short, rare meetings plus protected individual deep-work time, and warns that burnout from late nights and early mornings is common.

### Cited Findings
- UK (older): teams usually meet for about one hour a week as an "F1 Club", and "a minimum of an hour a week across the first and second half terms is recommended" before the regional final. **[Medium]** — [Teachwire](https://www.teachwire.net/products/f1-in-schools-stem-programme/)
- First-hand critique of meetings (Sept 2020, older): meetings are where teams "believe they're making progress when they're not", often in large, distracting rooms producing low-quality work. Trehan classes meetings as "shallow work" and calls them energy-draining. **[Medium]** — [Pannya Trehan, Medium: Why team meetings in F1 in Schools are a waste of time](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/why-team-meeting-in-f1-in-schools-are-a-waste-of-time-how-to-truly-make-the-most-out-of-f1-in-f3a68cadaedf)
- His remedy: replace "constant meetings and shallow work" with "rare/short meetings and deep work". Rather than a "loud, distraction-filled collaborative classroom", let each student work "independently in a state of deep focus at times when they feel they can complete their highest quality work". **[Medium]** — [Trehan, meetings article](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/why-team-meeting-in-f1-in-schools-are-a-waste-of-time-how-to-truly-make-the-most-out-of-f1-in-f3a68cadaedf)
- Burnout: "an overwhelmingly large amount of F1 in Schools students burn out or lose interest during the competition". It typically follows "too many long nights, too many early mornings, and pushing oneself beyond the point which one feels comfortable working". Setting priorities and managing time are "vital". **[Medium]** — [Pannya Trehan, Medium: How to achieve more with less effort & burnout](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/how-to-achieve-more-in-f1-in-schools-with-less-effort-time-why-you-should-be-careful-of-burn-f6e20fe23539)
- Transferable practice from professional F1 and the Red Arrows (not STEM Racing-specific): "debriefs should be routine and not reserved for when things go wrong". **[Low relevance]** — [Ignition Performance](https://ignitionperformance.com/articles/debrief-to-improve-lessons-from-the-red-arrows-and-formula-1)

### Inferences
- The one hour a week figure is a floor for Entry and Development teams. Nothing found says what Professional-class or World Finals teams actually do, but the scope of three portfolios plus car, pit display and presentation suggests far more.
- A routine consistent with the sources: a short weekly sync or stand-up to update the task board and risk register; asynchronous micro-tasks tracked on a Kanban board (per Trehan); protected individual deep-work time; and routine debriefs after each test or event. This is a synthesis, not a documented team routine.

### Gaps
- No current (2023-27) first-hand sources found on lunchtime versus after-school sessions, holiday sprints, stand-ups, or how teams plan around exam periods (GCSE/A-level, IB, US AP/finals, Australian HSC/VCE). Reddit and Discord accounts were not found or could not be reached. The one recent first-hand season recap found, [Sethmi Ekanayake, 2024-25 season recap (Tesla STEM HS, USA)](https://sethmi.medium.com/stem-racing-f1-in-schools-experience-2024-2025-season-recap-1ae01742c911), could not be read.

## 5. Tools teams use to run the project

### Takeaway
The official Guide names generic tools: Word and Excel, Asana, JIRA, Microsoft Project, Miro, Monday, Smartsheet, Trello and projectmanager.com. At least one Brazilian team built its own web app for RACI, risk, resources and finance, and CFD tools such as SimScale and Ansys appear in design iteration. No reliable STEM Racing-specific first-hand evidence was found on Notion, Discord, WhatsApp, Microsoft Teams or Google Workspace, or on what teams like or dislike about any tool.

## 6. Iteration management: design-make-test loops, machine time, test data, design freeze, versions

### Takeaway
The standard loop is Design–Analyse–Make–Test–Race: virtual wind-tunnel (CFD) analysis before the first prototype, then physical wind-tunnel and track testing, with CNC machining for Development and Professional cars and 3D printing for Entry cars. Research quantifies what iteration gains (e.g., a 9.89% drag reduction across CFD iterations). Hard submission dates act as de facto design freezes. No sourced detail was found on booking machine time or on file and version control.

### Cited Findings
- The loop is described as "Design-Analyze-Make-Test-Race". Each design is analysed with virtual wind-tunnel software before the first prototype, and the prototype is then tested in a real wind tunnel. Manufacturing starts from a block of material and uses 3D CAM software and CNC machining. **[Medium; older article]** — [Production Machining: SAE and F1 in Schools—Making a Difference](https://www.productionmachining.com/articles/sae-and-f1-in-schoolsmaking-a-difference)
- A peer-reviewed study evaluated a series of CFD design iterations, each change "targeting airflow obstructions and flow separation". The changes cut drag force by **9.89%**, and the authors stress "the importance of iterative design processes". **[Medium]** — [Journal of Applied Engineering Design and Simulation (UiTM): Optimising F1 in Schools car design using CFD and wind tunnel testing](https://jaeds.uitm.edu.my/index.php/jaeds/article/view/77)
- Role definitions build iteration in. The Design Engineer is expected to generate "many iterations of designs and testing concepts". The Manufacturing Engineer delivers "all physical prototype cars and components for testing, and spare parts development". **[Medium; older]** — [Roles on an F1 in Schools Team](https://f1inschoolsna.squarespace.com/s/Roles-on-an-F1-in-Schools-Team.pdf)
- Manufacturing method varies by class (USA). **[Medium]** — [STEM Racing USA: Competition Classes](https://www.stemracing.us/competition-classes); [STEM Racing Canada: Competition Classes](https://www.stemracing.ca/competition-classes)
  - Entry Class races 3D-printed cars.
  - Development and Professional classes build CNC-manufactured cars.
- Fixed submission gaps (see Q1). **[Medium]**
  - USA regional: car due 25 Nov, project elements due a week later on 2 Dec — [STEM Racing USA dates](https://www.stemracing.us/dates-registration-fees)
  - World Finals 2025: portfolios due 14 Sep, event from 28 Sep — [World Finals Regulations 2025](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)
- New technical regulations come out before each season. The Development Technical Regulations 2026-2027 file is dated around June 2026, judging by its upload timestamp. **[Low–Medium]** — [Development Technical Regulations 2026-2027 (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d66b2c057810a68895f00/1780311730822/STEM+Racing+Development+Technical+Regulations+2026-2027.pdf)

### Inferences
- Plan backwards from the car-submission date. Freeze external aerodynamics early enough to allow for:
  - CNC machining, with queueing if a shared school machine is used
  - finishing, painting and decals
  - weighing and dimensional checks against the technical regulations
  - building spares

  This is standard practice implied by the roles and deadlines. No source gives specific week counts.
- Documentation follows the car in the USA: project elements are due a week after the car. The documented design must match the submitted car, so late changes create rework.
- Recording each CFD run, wind-tunnel test and track test with its date, design version and measured change (as in the 9.89% study) is both good engineering and direct evidence of steady progress for judges.

### Gaps
- No sources found on how teams book CNC routers or 3D printers, on regional manufacturing-centre deadlines, on CAD file naming or version control, or on test-data logging templates.

## 7. Common failures and what prevents them

### Takeaway
Failure modes that could be sourced:
- burnout and loss of interest
- ineffective planning (Gantt charts drawn up without understanding the competition's phases)
- unproductive whole-team meetings
- a weak design process and unprofessional portfolios
- delegating work nobody else understands
- late submissions (a 20-point penalty at the World Finals)

Other causes the brief suspected (sponsor delays, machine access, exam periods, lost files, one person doing everything) could not be sourced in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Burnout and loss of interest are "very normal"; prevention means setting priorities, managing time, and avoiding "too many long nights, too many early mornings". **[Medium; 2020]** — [Trehan: burnout article](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/how-to-achieve-more-in-f1-in-schools-with-less-effort-time-why-you-should-be-careful-of-burn-f6e20fe23539)
- Planning theatre: most planning is "ineffective and an active waste of time", and students make Gantt charts "without knowing what each phase/step of the competition entails". The fix is a WBS plus micro-step to-do lists prioritised by urgency, size, importance and relevance. **[Medium; 2020]** — [Trehan: Gantt article](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/why-you-will-never-have-enough-time-in-f1-in-schools-and-gantt-charts-wont-help-65b58800d24d); [Trehan: WBS](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-use-work-breakdown-structure-f1-schools-pannya-trehan)
- Meetings that feel productive but are not; the fix is short, rare meetings and individual deep work. **[Medium; 2020]** — [Trehan: meetings article](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/why-team-meeting-in-f1-in-schools-are-a-waste-of-time-how-to-truly-make-the-most-out-of-f1-in-f3a68cadaedf)
- Lessons teams report: needing "a better design process", being "more professional in portfolios", and "truly understanding work being delegated" so they can help teammates. **[Low: attribution unclear, most likely the Ansys student-team blog]** — [Ansys blog](https://www.ansys.com/blog/formula-1-student-team)
- Deadline discipline has a direct cost: late World Finals portfolio submission carries a **20-point penalty**. **[Medium–High]** — [World Finals Regulations 2025](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)
- Over-length portfolios are not read in full. Judges "will only review the first 5/7 pages" of a portfolio that exceeds the limit. **[Medium]** — [World Finals Regulations 2025 (Brazil-hosted copy)](https://static.portaldaindustria.com.br/media/filer_public/8c/fd/8cfdb641-28a4-41a1-8cd5-2a46f6c4c052/regulamento_da_competicao.pdf); [STEM Racing USA & Canada Regulations 2024-25](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5d82b3ff7c2dda76efc9a139/t/67721fa157763069f3a5d05a/1735532453635/F1iS-NACompetitionRegulations2024_25_REV2.pdf)
- Transferable practice: routine debriefs after successes as well as failures "keep the learning loop alive". **[Low relevance]** — [Ignition Performance](https://ignitionperformance.com/articles/debrief-to-improve-lessons-from-the-red-arrows-and-formula-1)

### Inferences
- Strong teams appear to prevent slippage by:
  - making the competition's phases explicit (WBS) before scheduling
  - running work as small, owned tasks
  - protecting focused work time
  - building buffers before hard submission dates, which cost points or cannot be moved
  - debriefing after every test or event

  This synthesises the sourced points above and is not a documented playbook.
- "One person doing everything" connects to the delegation lesson and to the RACI practice in Q2–Q3: a RACI with named backups, plus shared files, reduces the damage when a key member is unavailable. This is inferred.

### Gaps
- No sources found on sponsor or payment delays, machine or workshop access, exam-period collapses, lost or corrupted files, or teams disbanding mid-season. Searches for first-hand failure accounts (Reddit and team blogs) either returned nothing relevant or could not be run before the search budget ran out.

## 8. What PM judging rewards and how teams show steady progress

### Takeaway
Judging uses published scorecards with KPIs, set against a short PM portfolio: up to 5 pages in the 2025-26 Dev/Pro regulations, A3 at the World Finals, Tabloid in the USA. Evidence therefore has to be dense, visual and dated. The official handbook presents PM as tracking progress and building evidence. Which PM elements are judged, and in which portfolio, varies by class and country. The individual scorecard lines could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Scorecards list the KPIs judges use when awarding points. The PM handbook helps teams "track progress effectively, and build strong evidence for Project Management judging". **[Medium]** — [STEM Racing Global: PMIEF](https://www.stemracing.com/partners/pmief)
- USA & Canada 2024-25: the PM portfolio should be Tabloid (11"×17") or similar and "detail the team's project management processes". Teams should "read the Project Management scorecard carefully to ensure that all areas to be assessed are included". **[Medium]** — [STEM Racing USA & Canada Competition Regulations 2024-25](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5d82b3ff7c2dda76efc9a139/t/67721fa157763069f3a5d05a/1735532453635/F1iS-NACompetitionRegulations2024_25_REV2.pdf)
- World Finals 2025: one digital and two physical copies of the PM portfolio, A3 (or equivalent). Requirements are in Articles C5, C6 and C7 together with the D&E, PM and Enterprise scorecards. **[Medium]** — [World Finals Regulations 2025](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)
- Page limits conflict across sources. **[Medium; conflicting]**
  - One summary of the 2025-26 Dev/Pro regulations (probably the UK operator's, as the file sits on the same host as the UK primary guidebook from F1 in Schools Ltd) gives the PM portfolio "a maximum of 5 pages in hard copy format" — [Competition Regulations 2025-2026 Dev/Pro](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6936b74056d414042f3a6e33/1765193536812/stem_racing_competition_regulations_2025-2026_dev_pro+(1).pdf); [UK primary guidebook 2025-26](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/692ec63c9a95275e6eeab1dc/1764673084854/stem_racing_competition_guidebook_2025-2026_primary+(1).pdf)
  - Another summary says judges review only "the first 5/7 pages" — [World Finals regs (Brazil copy)](https://static.portaldaindustria.com.br/media/filer_public/8c/fd/8cfdb641-28a4-41a1-8cd5-2a46f6c4c052/regulamento_da_competicao.pdf)
  - A team site says PM "is scored by a portfolio half the length of the other two" — [Hayabusa Racing](https://www.hayabusaracing.com/about-f1-in-schools)
- Which classes need a PM portfolio (USA). **[Medium]** — [STEM Racing USA: Competition Classes](https://www.stemracing.us/competition-classes)
  - Professional Class: Engineering, Enterprise *and* Project Management portfolios, plus a 10-minute verbal presentation.
  - Development Class: Engineering and Enterprise portfolios only, plus a full-scale pit display (3'×8'×8') and a 10-minute presentation.
  - Entry Class: a simplified engineering portfolio, a tabletop pit display and a 5-minute presentation.
- UK: one summary says a Project Management Guidebook helps Development and Professional teams gain higher marks for the *Enterprise* portfolio. - Portfolio presentation advice: optimise for judges' reading with charts and diagrams and concise language. A PM professional and judge explains on the Off The Track podcast how to document PM "to obtain the maximum amount of points". Off The Track also recorded feedback from judges in every category at the 2023 World Finals (older). **[Medium]** — [Off The Track podcast: How to do Project Management](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/offthetrack/episodes/How-to-do-Project-Management-in-F1-in-Schools-e2efbo4); [Off The Track: Learn](https://offthetrack.net/learn/); [Off The Track on Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/off-the-track/id1484587640)

### Inferences
- With about 5 pages for the whole PM story, the most efficient evidence of steady progress is:
  - dated schedule snapshots (baseline versus actual)
  - a risk register showing status changes over time
  - a budget and resource ledger
  - an iteration table (date, version, test, measured result)
  - short lessons-learned notes

  Each item shows *process over time*, which matches the Guide's framing ("track progress", "continuous improvement"). This is inferred; the specific scorecard lines were not confirmed.
- Because requirements differ by class (USA Development has no separate PM portfolio; USA Professional and the World Finals do), teams moving up a class need to start keeping PM records from day one of the season. They cannot reconstruct them at the end.
