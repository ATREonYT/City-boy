# Subtract Noise, Protect Students, Charge Only Adults

No big app does SubOne's whole job. The best of them do agree on how it should be done, though, and they suggest SubOne's biggest gains now come from removing things rather than adding them. Linear, Basecamp and Notion show that calm comes from opinionated defaults and repeated rounds of cutting back. Youth-team apps such as Heja, TeamSnap, Spond and Remind show that a whole team can use a product free while one adult pays per team, and that no adult should message a child privately. Coaching apps such as Track Titan, Coach Dave Delta and WHOOP show that data earns attention only when it is boiled down to one specific next step, backed by the underlying numbers. For SubOne, this means cutting Today from about 17 blocks to five modules that fit on one laptop screen, merging its two activity feeds into one, and keeping the same frame in every section: a dimmed, grouped sidebar, one header layout, ⌘K search, and a phone bar with four labelled sections plus More. Digests and quiet hours should be the default. In a randomised trial, batching notifications three times a day beat both constant alerts and silence, and teenagers already receive a median of 237 notifications a day. SubOne's refusal of streaks, points and leaderboards is an asset, not a handicap: EU guidance now expects such features to be off by default for minors, and UK advertising rules make any upsell aimed at students risky. The model of a free team with an adult paying is right. Plain freemium, though, usually converts only 3–5% of accounts, the market holds thousands of teams rather than millions, and paying £9 a month for a six-month UK season costs less than the £69 pass. Revenue therefore has to come from a 12-month season pass paid by invoice, a payment link sponsors can use, a school or trust tier, and deals with competition organisers. Confidence in these findings is moderate. The design principles rest on consistent guidance from W3C, GOV.UK, Apple, Material and Nielsen Norman Group, but most sources were read through search summaries, most numeric thresholds are expert guidance rather than tested cut-offs, and the conversion benchmarks come from business software that earns far more per customer than SubOne does.

## No app does SubOne's job, but a dozen each solve one piece

Student racing and robotics teams currently run on a patchwork of tools. FIRST names **Discord, Slack and Google Classroom** as the standard communication tools for its robotics teams ([FIRST](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/team-org.pdf?hsLang=en)). STEM Racing portfolios show teams adding Trello for sign-offs and calendars ([Rapidity Racing portfolio](https://d347awuzx0kdse.cloudfront.net/nationalpipeline/content-file/T50_RAPIDITYRACING_ENTERPRISEPORTFOLIO_PDF%20%282%29.pdf?v=3c90a95a2ec3b7c6d0158c214db526b68c6cb980)), and one FRC team built its own combined scouting and task tool, Lookout ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/introducing-lookout-for-frc-your-comprehensive-team-management-solution/452666)). More than convenience is at stake: the F1 in Schools project-management guide, written with the Project Management Institute's education foundation, states that **"project management is now an assessed part of the competition"** ([PMI](https://www.pmi.org/pmi-educational-foundation/about-us/news/the-impact-of-the-pmief-and-f1-in-schools-partnership)).

Mainstream tools fit this audience badly:

| Tool | Limit that hurts school teams | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Notion | Free education plan: **"K–12 students and educators are not eligible"** | [Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-for-education) |
| Discord | Users must be **at least 13** | [Discord](https://discord.com/terms) |
| Trello | Free workspaces **capped at 10 collaborators since 2024** | [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/trello/new-collaborator-limits) |
| Slack | Free plan shows **90 days** of history, and since August 2024 deletes anything older than a year. That destroys the early-season evidence a portfolio needs | [Slack Help](https://slack.com/help/articles/27204752526611-Feature-limitations-on-the-free-version-of-Slack) |

SubOne's real competitors are therefore free consumer tools, not discounted business software. Its opening is a workspace that is safe for schools, keeps a whole season's history and understands the competition.

**Linear** is the closest model for SubOne's craft:
- **Opinionated workflow.** Linear argues that flexible software "lets everyone invent their workflows, which creates chaos as teams scale" ([Figma blog on the Linear Method](https://www.figma.com/blog/the-linear-method-opinionated-software/)).
- **Speed.** It keeps data on the device, so navigation needs "no spinners, no loading states" ([performance.dev](https://performance.dev/how-is-linear-so-fast-a-technical-breakdown)).
- **Triage.** Anything arriving from outside a team lands in a **Triage** inbox, where someone accepts, declines or snoozes it ([Linear Docs](https://linear.app/docs/triage)). That is a ready-made home for mentor suggestions, judges' feedback and the race engineer's recommendations.
- **Calm is maintained, not achieved once.** A March 2024 redesign cut "visual noise and clutter" ([changelog](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-03-20-new-linear-ui)). A December 2024 update let people hide and reorder sidebar items ([changelog](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-12-18-personalized-sidebar)). A March 2026 refresh called "A calmer interface for a product in motion" dimmed the sidebar, used fewer icons and made headers the same everywhere ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)).

**Basecamp** builds calm into its defaults rather than leaving it to settings:
- Quiet hours ("Work Can Wait") are on by default, 8am to 6pm ([Signal v. Noise](https://signalvnoise.com/svn3/basecamp-3-work-can-wait/)).
- One "Hey!" menu collects nearly every notification ([Signal v. Noise](https://signalvnoise.com/posts/3955-a-preview-of-whats-new-in-basecamp-3)).
- Automatic check-ins replace status meetings.
- Hill charts show whether a piece of work is still being figured out or is simply being carried out ([Basecamp](https://basecamp.com/hill-charts)).

Three other work tools each add one lesson:
- **Notion** shows one set of data as tabs of table, board, calendar and timeline views, each with its own filters ([Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/views-filters-and-sorts)). It fixed its blank-page problem by asking new users "What will you use Notion for?" and preloading matching templates ([OnboardMe](https://onboardme.substack.com/p/how-notion-solved-the-blank-page-product-strategy-deepdive)).
- **Asana** separates "my work" from team work. It also shows what noise costs: forum users report four emails for a single action ([Asana Forum](https://forum.asana.com/t/way-too-many-email-notifications-going-to-my-team-despite-adjustments-in-the-notification-tab/579459)).
- **Figma** pins each comment to the exact point on the canvas it refers to, and comments are resolved from there ([Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041547593-View-and-manage-comments)).

The warning comes from the "everything apps". A review summary attributed to G2's editors, quoted through an aggregator and not independently checked, describes ClickUp as "buttons everywhere, sidebars, menus, settings, tabs" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/clickup/reviews?page=8)). SubOne's 11 sections plus an AI assistant will drift that way without discipline.

Youth-team apps supply SubOne's business model:
- Heja is "free for everyone on the team to use", while Team Pro is "priced per team" so "the entire team benefits from a single payment" ([Heja Help](https://help.heja.io/en/articles/3727834-heja-free-vs-team-pro-or-max)).
- GameChanger sells a Team Pass as "a one-time purchase that last[s] for an entire season" ([GameChanger](https://gc.com/pricing/team-pass)).
- Spond charges nothing and earns only from payment processing ([Spond Help](https://help.spond.com/app/en/articles/118091-payments-costs-in-the-spond-app)).

They also cut out chasing people. TeamSnap members answer yes, no or maybe straight from a reminder email ([TeamSnap](https://helpme.teamsnap.com/article/95-set-availability-from-automatic-reminder-emails)). Google Classroom emails guardians a daily or weekly summary of missing work, upcoming work and class activity ([Classroom Help](https://support.google.com/edu/classroom/answer/6386354?hl=en)).

Performance and sim-racing apps show how to turn raw data into one action:
- WHOOP's Recovery score boils several body signals down to one number while showing what went into it ([WHOOP](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/how-does-whoop-recovery-work-101/)).
- TrainingPeaks colours each session by how much of the plan was done, and uses grey for useful work nobody planned ([TrainingPeaks Help](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/204861204-Workout-Card-Overview)).
- Track Titan shows "the single biggest area of time loss across your lap" ([Track Titan](https://www.tracktitan.io/post/november-2025-update-coaching-flows)).
- Coach Dave Delta tells a driver "you're braking too early into turn 5" instead of reporting that a sector was slow ([Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/documentation/how-to-use-auto-insights-ai-coaching-in-delta/)).
- Hudl links every statistic to the video that produced it ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/products/assist)).
- The robotics site Statbotics publishes its own accuracy: its rating picks **70%** of match winners, against 65% for a baseline that uses win records alone ([Statbotics](https://www.statbotics.io/blog/models)).

The failures are as instructive. Forbes said Strava's AI summaries "promise insights but provide bland pep talks" ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/cyrusfarivar/2024/10/12/strava-upsets-fans-with-new-ai-feature-that-promises-insights-but-provides-bland-pep-talks/)). TechRadar listed "11 insights that prove Garmin has no right to charge for its AI subscription feature" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/garmins-new-subscription-ai-feature-is-hilariously-bad-so-far)).

|---|---|---|
| Linear | Opinionated defaults, instant speed from on-device data, ⌘K, repeated cuts to noise | Fixed status flows, a triage inbox for outside input, regular "noise audits" |
| Basecamp | Calm by default: quiet hours on, one inbox, check-ins, hill charts | Default quiet hours; a weekly check-in that doubles as portfolio evidence; honest progress states |
| Notion | One set of data shown in many views; onboarding that starts from templates | A view switcher in Plan, a role question at sign-up, and no section that ever opens empty |

| Slack, Discord | Where student teams already talk | Compete on safety, permanent history and links to work, not on voice or social features |
| Figma | Presence, and comments pinned to the exact spot | Comments on a part, a point on the 3D model or a portfolio page that resolve into evidence |
| TeamSnap, Heja, GameChanger, Spond | Free for the team, with one adult paying per team or per season | A per-team season pass; one-tap replies from a reminder |
| Remind, ClassDojo, Seesaw, Google Classroom | Safeguarding fit for schools, and digests for guardians | No private adult–student chat, a chat log nobody can edit, approval before anything goes public |

| Track Titan, Coach Dave Delta, RaceChrono | The single biggest time loss, measured against a reference lap | A race engineer that names one cause and one fix, measured against the team's best run |
| Hudl | Every number linked to its video | Link each run to its car version, clip and notes |

## Calm comes from subtraction, and Today needs the most of it

People judge an interface almost instantly, and the judgement sticks. In a 2013 study, 548 volunteers rated 450 websites after seeing each for 500 milliseconds. Perceived visual complexity and colourfulness, together with the raters' backgrounds, explained **about half the variation in how appealing the sites looked**. The authors found this first impression later shaped judgements of usability and trustworthiness ([Reinecke et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2470654.2481281)). A Google study found the best first impressions came from low visual complexity combined with high "prototypicality", meaning the page looked like a typical example of its kind ([Tuch et al.](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/)).

Teenagers are even harsher judges. Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g) studied 100 teens aged 13–17 and found that they:
- "have a strong appreciation for aesthetics but detest sites that appear cluttered and contain pointless multimedia";
- dislike tiny text "as much as adults do";
- struggle with drag-and-drop, dropdown menus and small buttons on touch screens.

Source: [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/).

Calm is not the same as bland, though. W3C's cognitive-accessibility guidance (COGA) describes a good screen as one where **"the main feature is much bigger than anything else"**, and recommends **five or fewer main choices per screen** ([W3C COGA](https://github.com/w3c/coga/blob/main/design-guide/o5p03-manageable-quantity.html)). The lesson for SubOne is to concentrate emphasis on one thing rather than remove it. SubOne should also keep its conventional layout of sidebar, header and content, because familiarity is an asset.

### What Linear's calmer redesign changed

Linear's March 2026 refresh is the most concrete recipe available for a dense product ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). It:
- made the navigation sidebar "a few notches dimmer, allowing the main content area—where users work—to take precedence";
- reduced icon use, shrank the icons and removed "colored team icon backgrounds";
- made "headers, navigation, and view controls" the same across every area;
- moved from a cool blue-grey to "a warmer gray that still feels crisp, but less saturated".

Linear's 2024 colour system maps almost exactly onto SubOne's single team colour. Instead of 98 hand-set colour settings, Linear builds every theme from **three inputs (base colour, accent colour and contrast)**. It uses a colour model called LCH, in which equal steps look equally different to the eye, and the contrast input produces high-contrast themes automatically ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)).

### Use strong colour only for exceptions

Design systems also limit how much colour and status a screen carries:
- IBM's Carbon design system warns that "more than five or six indicators can overwhelm users". It says to use plain text where no action is needed, and to pair colour with a shape or a word ([Carbon](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon-website/blob/d8783ad2ae3b5e59c58f58311491f8a2c4e62631/src/pages/patterns/status-indicator-pattern/index.mdx)).
- GOV.UK advises teams to "start with the smallest number of statuses you think might work" ([GOV.UK](https://github.com/alphagov/govuk-design-system/blob/main/src/components/tag/index.md)).

For SubOne, that means using strong colour only for exceptions:

| Status | How to show it |
|---|---|
| On track | Plain grey text, no colour |
| At risk, needs a decision | Amber |
| Blocked or overdue, needs you | Red |

Every coloured status should also carry an icon and a word, with at least 3:1 contrast against its background.

### Motion, themes and personal settings

**Motion.** W3C notes that "there is no five second exception for auto-updating" content, and that blinking content distracts people with attention difficulties ([WCAG](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/understanding/20/pause-stop-hide.html)). So:
- live race timing needs a pause control;
- the 3D car viewer should not spin on its own;
- celebratory motion belongs only to rare, real milestones, such as the car passing the rules check.

**Themes.** Light mode should stay the default. In research summarised by NN/g, "light mode won across all dimensions" of visual sharpness and proofreading for both younger and older adults, and its advantage grew as text got smaller ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)). That matters for tables of run times. SubOne should still offer System, Light and Dark, with the dark theme built from dark greys rather than pure black ([Material](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Dark.md)).

**Accessibility.** The UK Home Office's accessibility posters give specific advice:
- For autistic users: "use simple colours" and "build simple and consistent layouts" ([autism poster](https://github.com/UKHomeOffice/accessibility-posters/blob/cce566a3dc15d0d359697132ae5764c1fc0b9066/autism.html)).
- For dyslexic users: "let users change the contrast between background and text", because many are "sensitive to bright, high contrast colours" ([dyslexia poster](https://github.com/UKHomeOffice/accessibility-posters/blob/cce566a3dc15d0d359697132ae5764c1fc0b9066/dyslexia.html)).

**Settings.** Two additions serve these users and cost little once all colours are defined as shared design values rather than set by hand:
- A Settings panel with Theme, Contrast, Motion, Density and a "reduce colour" option.
- A Focus mode that hides badges, chat previews and AI suggestions for a set time.

### Today should be a one-screen answer, not a five-screen feed

SubOne's current Today screen breaks nearly every rule above. Stephen Few's classic definition of a dashboard is information "consolidated and arranged on a single screen so the information can be monitored at a glance". He warns that "if you must scroll around to see all the information, it has transgressed the boundaries of a dashboard" ([Few](https://github.com/noah-goodrich/borg-collective/blob/9738fd86895746d5e7430235f5ff9d5126cc75dd/docs/infoviz/research/sources/operational-few-dashboard-design.md)). Against that standard, Today has three problems:
- **Too many blocks.** About 17 blocks stacked across roughly five phone screens is several times COGA's limit of five main choices.
- **Duplicate feeds.** Two feeds repeat the same team changes, which doubles the noise.
- **Competing prompts.** Several "start here" prompts compete, so nothing stands out. GOV.UK's banner rule applies directly: "Avoid showing more than one notification banner on the same page. Instead, combine the messages" ([GOV.UK](https://github.com/alphagov/govuk-design-system/blob/main/src/components/notification-banner/index.md)).

GitHub separates notifications about things you take part in from things you only watch ([GitHub Docs](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/main/content/subscriptions-and-notifications/get-started/configuring-notifications.md)). A useful test follows from that split: each new event should appear live in exactly one place, and anywhere else only in summaries.

The fix is to make Today a status screen that answers two questions: "what should I do now, and is anything wrong?" Deeper analysis moves into the sections, as Carbon recommends for this kind of dashboard ([Carbon](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon-website/blob/d8783ad2ae3b5e59c58f58311491f8a2c4e62631/src/pages/data-visualization/dashboards/index.mdx)). Other big apps point the same way: Garmin's Morning Report is a single summary card, and GitHub's home screen shows only "up to 4 updates made in the last two weeks" ([Garmin wiki](https://wiki.garminrumors.com/Morning_Report); [GitHub Docs](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/main/content/account-and-profile/reference/personal-dashboard.md)).

| Module | What it holds | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Your next move | The one task SubOne picks for this person, with owner, due date and an optional "when" | Largest element on the screen; done and snooze work in place |
| 2. Needs you | Tasks assigned to me, @mentions, approvals | The only module with number badges |
| 3. Next milestone | Countdown to the next competition deadline, with checklist progress | Every number has context ("£1.2k left of £2k") |
| 4. Since you were last here | One merged team feed of 3–5 meaningful changes | Replaces both current feeds; "See all" opens Activity |
| 5. Ask the race engineer | A prompt box with at most two suggestions based on the team's own data | Never pops up; opens only when the user asks |

The optional "when" draws on research on "implementation intentions", meaning plans that say when and where you will act. A meta-analysis of 94 tests found these plans had a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals (d ≈ 0.65). This figure comes from the research notes' recollection of the literature and was not re-checked ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601%2806%2938002-1)).

The rest of Today changes as follows:
- **New teams** see a single setup checklist in place of module 4 until setup is done, and every other "start here" prompt goes. The checklist should start with finished steps already ticked ("Team created", "Competition chosen"), which uses the "endowed progress" effect. In a field experiment, also recalled rather than re-checked, a car-wash loyalty card with two of ten stamps pre-filled was completed by 34% of customers, against 19% for an empty eight-stamp card ([Nunes & Drèze](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)).
- **Recent and pinned items** move to the sidebar and the ⌘K palette.
- **Charts** move to the Race and Money sections.
- **Teachers and mentors** get a different default, led by approvals and team balance.

The result should fit on one laptop screen without scrolling. On a phone, the same modules stack in the same order, so the next move and anything that needs the student appear on the first screen.

## Eleven sections work when the frame never moves

The evidence favours navigation that is visible and broad rather than hidden and deep. In a 2016 NN/g study of 179 participants on six websites, desktop users used hidden "hamburger" navigation in **27%** of cases, against 48% for visible navigation and 50% for a combination of the two. On mobile, the figures were **57% for hidden against 86% for combined**. Hiding navigation "cut almost in half" how often people discovered it, and made tasks take **39% longer on desktop and 15% longer on mobile** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). An older experiment arranged 512 items as 8×8×8, 16×32 and 32×16 menus. It found the broad, shallow 16×32 layout fastest and the deepest 8×8×8 layout slowest ([Larson & Czerwinski](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/trs/99-15/99-15.html)).

SubOne has 11 sections, each with 3–6 tabs, which gives about 50 destinations across two levels. That is the shape this evidence supports. The rule to protect is: never add a third level. The hamburger data is a decade old and comes from content websites rather than logged-in team apps, but no newer study contradicts it.

### A laptop sidebar that recedes but never hides

Apple's sidebar guidance sets the laptop rules ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)):
- "Show no more than two levels of hierarchy in a sidebar."
- "Let people customize the contents of a sidebar."
- "Avoid hiding the sidebar by default."

Recent redesigns show both the good and the bad directions:
- **Linear** now lets people hide and reorder sidebar items, and choose a dot or a count for unread items ([Linear](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-12-18-personalized-sidebar)).
- **Slack**'s 2023 redesign added a single Activity view that combines mentions, threads and reactions ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/slacks-latest-redesign-has-a-dedicated-dm-tab-and-a-discord-style-activity-view-130032154.html)).
- **Figma** tried floating side panels in its 2024 redesign, then went back to fixed panels because the floating ones cramped the canvas ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/our-approach-to-designing-ui3/)).

SubOne's sidebar should run top to bottom as follows:
1. A compact team switcher.
2. A visible "Search or jump to… ⌘K" field and a "+ New" button.
3. A personal block: Today, Inbox and My tasks.
4. Up to about seven pinned items.
5. Three collapsible groups:
   - **Build:** Plan, Car, Race.
   - **Enterprise:** Money, Marketing, Pitch, Portfolio.
   - **People:** Chat, Team.
6. Settings and the profile at the foot.

Students can hide sections they never use. Hidden sections remain reachable through ⌘K. Teachers set the default layout for each role. On narrow windows the sidebar collapses to a rail of icons; on a laptop it never turns into a hamburger menu.

### A phone bar of four labelled sections plus More

On phones, platform guidance sets tight limits:
- Material's navigation bars "can have three to five destinations" ([Material](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/BottomNavigation.md)).
- Apple asks for single-word labels, and says to "use a tab bar to support navigation, not to provide actions" and to "reserve badges for critical information" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)).
- Apple also warns that a "More" tab "makes it harder for people to reach and notice content", and suggests that apps with many sections let people choose which ones appear in the bar ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)).

Discord shows the cost of getting this wrong. It split servers and messages into separate mobile tabs in 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/05/discord-app-redesign-mobile)). A later redesign reportedly drew a backlash because it added taps for people who switch channels "dozens of times per session" ([remio.ai](https://www.remio.ai/post/discord-redesign-backlash-reveals-mobile-app-tradeoffs)).

SubOne's phone navigation should therefore work like this:
- **Bar:** four labelled sections plus a labelled More, all at least 44 px tall.
- **Default sections:** Today, Plan, Chat, and Car or Race. There is no usage data yet, so confirm these with analytics.
- **Personal choice:** each person can swap which sections appear.
- **Stability:** the bar never changes with the season.
- **More:** opens a list grouped exactly like the desktop sidebar, with search at the top.
- **Actions stay out of the bar:** "+ New" and "Ask race engineer" are actions, so they live in the screen header.
- **Race day:** a slim strip above the bar can hold a live race-day status or an active timer. This follows Apple's own iOS 26 "accessory" pattern.

### Tabs for different content, a switcher for different views

NN/g's rule for in-page tabs is "the fewer tabs, the better" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tabs-used-right/)). GitHub's design system adds that "each tab panel should have discrete content with a unique URL, not just different formats to view the same content" ([Primer](https://github.com/primer/design/blob/main/content/components/underline-nav.mdx)).

Plan's six tabs currently mix two kinds of thing:
- **Separate content:** Playbook, Risks, Workload.
- **Different formats of the same schedule:** Calendar, Timeline, Board.

Folding the three formats into one "Schedule" tab with a view switcher cuts Plan to four tabs. Car's versions should use a list with a detail pane. The 3D viewer and the rules check are tools, so they belong on each car version rather than as sibling tabs.

Two more rules keep the frame stable:
- **Addresses for every view.** Every view should have its own web address, such as `/plan/schedule?view=board&filter=mine`, so links pasted in Chat or produced by the race engineer open exactly that state. SubOne should also remember each person's last view.
- **One header layout.** Every section should use the same header, with title, main action, view switcher and filters always in the same places, as in Linear's 2026 refresh.

### A command palette that complements visible navigation

GitHub's command palette ([GitHub Docs](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/main/content/get-started/accessibility/github-command-palette.md)):
- "shows your location at the top left and uses it as the scope for suggestions";
- accepts short prefixes for people, issues and commands;
- lets users change the shortcut that opens it.

Superhuman shows each command's keyboard shortcut next to it, so users "learn the shortcut for next time" ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-to-build-a-remarkable-command-palette/)). Because many people browse rather than search, the palette should add to the visible sidebar, not replace it.

SubOne's palette should work like this:
- **Opening it:** show Ctrl+K on Windows and Chromebooks, and ⌘K on Macs.
- **Before anything is typed:** show recent items, actions for the current section, and all the destinations, with synonyms ("budget" finds Money, "sponsors" finds Marketing).
- **Results:** group them by type.
- **Last row:** always "Ask the race engineer: …" with the typed text.
- **Permissions:** respect role permissions, so a student never sees restricted Money records.
- **Shortcuts:** allow single-letter shortcuts only with an off switch. W3C warns that "single-key shortcuts are disastrous for speech users" ([W3C](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/understanding/21/character-key-shortcuts.html)).

### The lightest container that fits the edit

Rules from Apple, GitHub and Android point to one rule of thumb: use the lightest container that fits the task.
- Apple asks that modal tasks stay "simple, short, and streamlined", with no dialog opened on top of another ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality)).
- GitHub's design system says to "never mix save patterns in a single form", meaning autosave and a Save button should not appear together ([Primer](https://github.com/primer/design/blob/main/content/ui-patterns/saving.mdx)).
- Aza Raskin's rule is "never use a warning when you mean undo", because people click through routine confirmations without reading them ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/neveruseawarning/)).
- Android's layout guidance puts a supporting panel at about 30% of a wide screen ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/adaptive-apps/guides/canonical-layouts)). That suits the race engineer docked on the right.

| Task | Container on laptop | Container on phone |
|---|---|---|
| Change one field (status, owner, due date) | Inline, in the list or board | Inline, or a half-height sheet |
| Create a short record (5 fields or fewer) | Dialog with a Save button | Full-height sheet with Cancel and Save |
| Open a record from a list, board or calendar | Side panel that doesn't block the page, saving each field as you go and showing "Saving…/Saved" | Full page with a Back button |
| Work on rich records (car versions, pitch decks, portfolio pages, race-day plans) | Full page | Full page |
| Delete, archive, move | Act at once, then offer Undo; confirm only irreversible actions such as deleting a team or financial records | Same |
| Ask the race engineer | Panel docked on the right, about 30% wide | Full-height sheet |

### The same features at every screen size

Apple's rule is "don't change your app's functionality based on the space it occupies" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)). Android's window-size classes provide the breakpoints ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/use-window-size-classes)):

| Width (CSS px) | Typical device | Navigation | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 600 | Phones | Bottom bar, 4 sections plus More | One pane; records open as pages |
| 600–839 | Tablets upright, half-width laptop windows | Rail of icons with labels | List or detail |
| 840–1199 | Tablets sideways, small laptops | Collapsible sidebar | List and detail side by side; AI panel overlays |
| 1200 and up | Laptops, desktops | Full grouped sidebar | List, detail panel and docked race engineer |

The web-app plumbing matters because SubOne lives in a browser. Installing it works differently by platform:
- **Chrome, Edge and Android** can show a custom Install button, once the person has seen value.
- **iPhone** users need illustrated steps: Share, then "Add to Home Screen" ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Guides/Making_PWAs_installable)).

Push notifications reach an iPhone only when the web app has been added to the Home Screen ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)). The in-app inbox and email digests must therefore carry everything essential.

Three accessibility rules complete the frame:
- Sticky bars must never hide the element that has keyboard focus ([WCAG 2.4.11](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/understanding/22/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)).
- Dialogs must move keyboard focus in when they open and return it to where it was when they close ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/)).
- Every drag on a board needs a "Move to…" menu as an alternative, which also helps the teens NN/g saw struggle with drag-and-drop.

## Loved apps win on first-session value, speed and forgiveness

Beyond layout, the most admired products share a small set of habits that SubOne can copy almost directly. They show value before asking for commitment, respond within a tenth of a second, forgive mistakes, and treat people's attention as scarce.

### Show value before commitment, and teach in context

Onboarding evidence favours short, user-chosen guidance over long tours. Figures from Chameleon, which makes product-tour software, drawn from about 15 million tour interactions:

| Tour type | Completion rate |
|---|---|
| All tours (average) | **61%** |
| 3 steps | about 72% (read off a chart) |
| 7 steps | about 16% (read off a chart) |
| Launched by the user | reported as 123% higher than tours that launch automatically |

Sources: [Chameleon](https://www.chameleon.io/blog/product-tour-benchmarks-highlights); [tour-kit docs](https://github.com/domidex01/tour-kit). These are a vendor's correlational figures, and finishing a tour is not the same as adopting the product.

NN/g's guidance points the same way. Up-front tutorials tend to be skipped and forgotten, while help given in context works better, and empty screens should say what belongs there and offer a direct path to the key task ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)).

For a team product, success has to be defined at team level. Slack's founder said "any team that has exchanged 2,000 messages in its history has tried Slack — really tried it", and that 93% of those teams were still using it. He also said "it takes 3 to make it really work" ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/from-0-to-1b-slacks-founder-shares-their-epic-launch-strategy/)).

SubOne should take four things from this:
- **Set a team-level activation threshold and test it.** For example, within 14 days: at least three members have joined, the plan includes the competition date, at least one car version is logged, and at least one Today move is done. Then check which of these early behaviours predict teams still being active at their first competition.
- **Ask a few questions at sign-up.** Ask for role, competition class and region, as Notion asks new users what they will use it for, and preload a season plan with sample parts and sponsor stages so no section ever opens empty.
- **Let people try before signing up.** Offer a sample team workspace before sign-up, with a "Start my team from this" action that copies the structure but not the data.
- **Keep tours short and optional.** No tour chapter should run longer than three steps, and tours should start only from a "Show me how" link, never on their own.

For 11–13-year-olds, the teacher should create the team and students should join by code.

### Speed is felt at a tenth of a second

Response-time guidance sets three limits:
- **0.1 seconds** feels instant.
- **1 second** keeps the user's train of thought unbroken.
- **10 seconds** is about the limit of attention.

Source: [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/). Superhuman builds to a "100ms rule" ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/superhuman-is-built-for-speed/)).

Linear's sync engine shows how to reach that speed. It stores data in the browser, updates the screen immediately and queues each change for the server in the background. Linear's chief technology officer called an outsider's reverse-engineered write-up of the engine "probably the best documentation that exists" ([reverse-linear-sync-engine](https://github.com/wzhudev/reverse-linear-sync-engine)).

Google's Core Web Vitals give measurable targets, judged at the 75th percentile of page loads, so at least three in four loads must meet them:
- **Largest Contentful Paint** (how fast the main content appears): **2.5 seconds or less**.
- **Interaction to Next Paint** (how fast the page responds to a tap or click): **200 milliseconds or less**.
- **Cumulative Layout Shift** (how much the layout jumps while loading): **0.1 or less**.

Source: [web-vitals](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/web-vitals). SubOne should measure these itself, with no third-party tracker, on school Chromebooks and older Android phones.

Two cautions apply:
- **Speed and sales.** A Google and Deloitte study linked a 0.1-second faster mobile site with **8.4% more retail conversions** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions)). That comes from e-commerce and should not be scaled up to SubOne.
- **Skeleton screens.** Placeholder layouts shown while content loads do not reliably feel faster. In one test with 136 participants they did worst ([Viget](https://www.viget.com/articles/a-bone-to-pick-with-skeleton-screens/)). GitHub's design system advises no loading indicator at all for waits under one second ([Primer](https://github.com/primer/design/blob/main/content/ui-patterns/loading.mdx)).

The real fix is data that is already on the device. For the AI's slower answers, show the concrete steps it is taking rather than a bare spinner. In experiments on the "labour illusion", people valued a service more when they could see the work it was doing ([Buell & Norton](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1376)).

Offline use is a real advantage in workshops and at race venues with poor connections:
- Notion added offline mode only in August 2025, and with limits ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/releases/2025-08-19)).
- Robotics teams' own scouting tools run with neither Wi-Fi nor mobile data ([ScoutingPASS](https://github.com/PWNAGERobotics/ScoutingPASS)).

SubOne's run log and checklists should therefore work offline and show a quiet status: "Saved on this device, will sync when online".

### Forgiveness is cheap, and matters more when peers are watching

GOV.UK's rules for error messages ([GOV.UK](https://github.com/alphagov/govuk-design-system/blob/main/src/components/error-message/index.md)):
- "Describe what has happened and tell them how to fix it."
- Avoid "please", "sorry", "valid" and "invalid", and humour such as "oops".
- "Do not clear any form fields" when showing an error.

For SubOne, that means copy written for an 11-year-old reader, such as "Add a date for your first race" instead of "Invalid date".

Deleting tasks, runs, files and comments should be undoable, with a trash that keeps items for 30 days. Typed confirmation should be reserved for actions that cannot be undone and affect a lot of work, such as deleting a season, removing a member or transferring ownership. Car versions already give students the right mental model: anything can be restored to an earlier version. Easy recovery matters more for teenagers because teammates can see their mistakes in a shared space.

### Batch updates, but keep direct requests prompt

The strongest notification evidence is a randomised field experiment with **237** participants ([Fitz, Kushlev et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563219302596)). It compared three set-ups:

| Notification set-up | Result compared with the usual flow |
|---|---|
| Batched three times a day | Felt more attentive, productive and in control; better mood; lower stress |
| Switched off entirely | Few of those benefits; higher anxiety and fear of missing out |
| Usual flow (control) | Baseline |

The load is already heavy for both groups SubOne serves:
- **Students.** Common Sense Media tracked 203 young people aged 11–17 for a week. They received a median of **237 notifications a day**, about a quarter during the school day and 5% at night ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/teens-are-bombarded-with-hundreds-of-notifications-a-day)).
- **Teachers.** Microsoft's data shows workers interrupted by a meeting, email or chat about **every two minutes** during core hours ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday)).

Well-run products ration notifications on purpose:
- Duolingo's notification team could not increase the number of notifications "without strong justification and CEO approval" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)).
- GitHub separates notifications about things you take part in from things you only watch, and lets users set hours for mobile push ([GitHub Docs](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/main/content/subscriptions-and-notifications/get-started/configuring-notifications.md)).
- Apple's platform defines four urgency levels, from "passive", which appears silently in the notification list, to "critical", which sounds even when the phone is muted ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationinterruptionlevel)).

SubOne's defaults should follow the same pattern:

| Setting | Students | Teachers and mentors |
|---|---|---|
| Team activity | In-app inbox and digest only | Weekly email digest in three parts: overdue, due next week, what the team did (like Google Classroom's guardian summaries) |
| Real-time alerts | Only for things aimed at them: an assignment, a mention or a deadline within 48 hours | Only for approvals, safety flags and direct questions |
| Push | Off until the student opts in, asked in context after install | Optional |
| Quiet hours | 21:00–07:00 and school hours by default; a teacher can open the club's after-school slot or switch on a race-day mode | Optional |
| Push limit | At most one a day, except assignments due within 24 hours | No fixed limit |
| Badges | Numbers only for things that need action, capped at "9+"; a plain dot for anything else new | Same |
| Ignored reminders | Pause automatically, with neutral wording: "We've paused reminders. Turn them back on anytime." | Same |

California's SB 976 protects the same night and school-day hours for minors on social feeds ([SB 976](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB976)). SubOne very likely falls outside that law, but its hours make a sensible benchmark.

### Measure team progress, not screen time

Google's HEART framework gives a ready set of measures: happiness, engagement, adoption, retention and task success ([Rodden et al.](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/)).

The popular rule that five test users find about 85% of usability problems is an average with a wide spread. Random groups of five found between **55% and 99%** of problems ([Faulkner](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03195514)). SubOne should therefore run several small rounds of testing: separate rounds for students aged 11–13, 14–16 and 17–19, and 6–12 interviews with teachers before fixing digest, Pro and safeguarding decisions ([Guest et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1525822X05279903)).

Strava now boasts that its subscribers were active for an hour for every two minutes spent in the app ([Strava](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025)). That is the right model: count progress, not minutes. SubOne's lead measures should be:
- weekly active teams, meaning at least three members doing real work;
- retention by season-start group, judged against the competition calendar rather than calendar months;
- for each notification type, the share acted on within 24 hours and the share muted.

All of these can be counted by SubOne itself, at team level and in aggregate, without third-party trackers.

## Teen protections rule out the tricks that drive engagement elsewhere

The mechanics SubOne has refused do work, and that is exactly why they are now restricted for young users.

**The evidence that they work.** Duolingo's leaderboards raised "overall learning time... by 17%" and tripled the number of highly engaged learners. Users "were automatically opted in" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)).

**The rules that now restrict them:**
- The European Commission's July 2025 guidelines on protecting minors recommend switching off by default, for minors, features that drive excessive use, naming streaks, autoplay and push notifications ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)).
- The UK Children's Code tells services to "avoid using personal data in a way that incentivises children to stay engaged" and to present options to keep going "neutrally". This wording was read from a secondary copy of the code ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/)).

**The evidence of harm:**
- Studies of leaderboards in education find they work as negative feedback for low-ranked students, lowering their sense of competence and their motivation ([Li et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.13077)).
- Snapchat-style streaks are linked with problematic phone use and fear of missing out among young adolescents ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772503023000476)).
- A child-development professor called ClassDojo's public points "humiliating for children" ([Coda Story](https://www.codastory.com/authoritarian-tech/classdojo-schools-bias/)).

**The strongest case for points.** A meta-analysis of game elements in learning found small-to-medium gains: g = 0.49 for cognitive, 0.36 for motivational and 0.25 for behavioural outcomes. Competition combined with collaboration was especially effective for behaviour ([Sailer & Homner](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270)). So SubOne's stance does give up some engagement.

SubOne should accept that trade-off, for two reasons. First, the buyer is a teacher, whose trust depends on SubOne visibly not manipulating students. Second, a team product can measure success by team progress rather than by any one student's time in the app.

### What SubOne should use instead

SubOne should rely on mechanics that stay compatible with the code:

- **Progress, made visible.** A diary study of about 12,000 workday entries found that progress in meaningful work was the biggest everyday boost to motivation ([Amabile & Kramer](https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins); recalled rather than re-read this session).
- **Feedback about the work, not rewards.** Prefer "drag down 4% since version 3" over points.
- **Quiet thanks between teammates.** A light "nice work" reaction inside the team, with no totals per person. Strava research links receiving kudos with running more often ([Social Networks](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378873322000909)), but a 2026 study also found it brings comparison and stress ([Behavioral Sciences](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12938745/)).
- **Comparison only with the team's own past.** WHOOP, for example, compares each member's week with their own three-week average.
- **An end-of-season recap.** This is the strongest compatible mechanic:
  - *Scale:* Spotify Wrapped 2025 reached **200 million engaged users within 24 hours** ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-wrapped-campaign-hit-200m-engaged-users-in-24-hours-a-19-yoy-increase/)).
  - *Warning:* Strava drew complaints when it put its Year in Sport recap behind the subscription in December 2025 ([T3](https://www.t3.com/tech/dear-strava-we-have-a-paywall-problem-thats-gone-a-step-too-far)).
  - *SubOne's version:* team-level, private by default, shareable after teacher approval and free for every team. It would show runs logged, best time and how much it improved, car versions, sponsors won and events attended. It doubles as portfolio and sponsor-report material.

### Chat that a safeguarding lead can approve

Sport bodies and school apps agree on one rule: no private channel between an adult and a child.

**What the sport bodies require:**
- **US Center for SafeSport:** an adult's message to more than one minor must copy another adult or all the minors' parents ([SafeSport](https://uscenterforsafesport.org/how-to-safely-communicate-with-minor-athletes/)).
- **NSPCC Child Protection in Sport Unit (UK):** coaches should not message any child in an individual chat. Group chats with under-18s need two monitoring staff, both with DBS (criminal record) checks ([CPSU](https://thecpsu.org.uk/resource-library/tools/whatsapp-go-to-guide-for-sports/)).
- **FIRST robotics:** discourages direct messages through unofficial platforms ([FIRST YPP](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/youth-protection-program)).

**How the leading apps build it in:**
- **Remind:** under-13s can only receive messages, with a parent copied. No message can be edited or deleted, and history can be downloaded ([Remind](https://help.remind.com/hc/en-us/articles/203744719-Trust-and-safety-on-Remind)).
- **ClassDojo:** allowed one-to-one teacher–student messages only behind district, school and class switches, with automatic scanning ([ClassDojo](https://help.classdojo.com/hc/en-us/articles/42702434157069-Teacher-to-Student-Messaging-FAQ-For-Teachers-and-School-Leaders)).
- **Seesaw:** lets teachers approve students' posts before families see them ([Seesaw](https://help.seesaw.me/hc/en-us/articles/203008899-Privacy-for-student-journals)).
- **Spond:** registers young children with no email address, linked to a guardian ([Spond](https://help.spond.com/app/en/articles/301491-correct-registration-of-children-and-guardians-in-spond-app)).
- **Strava:** keeps under-18s off leaderboards and out of messaging ([Strava](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412328250893-Your-Privacy-Control-Defaults-When-You-re-Under-18-on-Strava)).

**The cost of getting it wrong.** In 2022 Epic Games agreed to pay **$245 million** over manipulative designs that led to unwanted purchases ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/12/fortnite-video-game-maker-epic-games-pay-more-half-billion-dollars-over-ftc-allegations)). A separate penalty under COPPA, the US children's privacy law, was reportedly tied partly to voice and text chat being switched on by default for children.

**SubOne's defaults should therefore be:**
- **No private adult–student threads.** No one-to-one thread between an adult and a student. Every thread with an adult and a student includes a second adult, or is visible to all the team's adults.
- **Guardian copies.** An optional "copy guardians" mode copies every student's guardian, never just some.
- **Permanent records.** A chat log that can't be edited but can be exported, plus a report button.
- **Hidden contact details.** No one's phone number or email is shown.
- **Approval before anything goes public.** A teacher or mentor approves anything that leaves the team: marketing posts, sponsor emails, public share links, and photos that show students.
- **Invite-only.** Workspaces are invite-only, with no public directory or search.
- **Visible vouching.** Show who vouched for each adult, for example "Teacher, confirmed by school admin".
- **Accounts for the youngest.** 11–12-year-olds get teacher-managed, email-free profiles linked to a guardian.
- **No pressure features.** No read receipts, typing indicators or "last seen" for students by default.

Two policy points remain open:
- **Adult students.** 18–19-year-old students are legally adults on teams with minors. SafeSport's logic suggests they should follow the adult rules.
- **Permanent decisions.** Chat also needs a permanent record of decisions. Basecamp keeps a message board for decisions alongside quick chat. SubOne needs a one-tap "mark as decision" that posts the outcome to Announcements, so it doesn't scroll away.

### An AI race engineer that is a tool, not a friend

Teenagers already treat chatbots as companions:
- **US:** a Common Sense Media survey of 1,060 teens aged 13–17 found **72%** have used AI companions, **33%** for social interaction or relationships, and **23%** trust them "quite a bit" or "completely" ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/talk-trust-and-trade-offs-how-and-why-teens-use-ai-companions)).
- **UK:** 64% of 9–17-year-olds use AI chatbots, and 35% say it feels like talking to a friend ([Internet Matters](https://www.internetmatters.org/hub/research/me-myself-and-ai-chatbot-research/)).

Regulation is tightening:
- **EU:** the AI Act's duty to tell people they are dealing with an AI applies from **2 August 2026** ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)). That date was not re-checked against later proposals to shift AI Act deadlines.
- **California:** SB 243 requires companion chatbots to remind known minors at least every three hours to take a break and that the bot is not human ([SB 243](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB243)). A task-focused race engineer probably falls outside the law, but the practice is a sensible default.
- **UK:** the Department for Education's safety expectations for generative AI in education products cover filtering, monitoring and reporting ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-expectations)).

Big vendors are cautious with this age group:
- Figma withholds its AI tools from school-age users ([Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041061214-Figma-for-Education)).
- Google gives under-18s a separate Gemini experience in Classroom. Teachers choose the materials it draws on, admins can switch it off, and Google says the data is not used to train its models ([Google](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/education/gemini-iste-2025/)).

**Design guidance for the race engineer:**
- **Microsoft's 18 guidelines for human–AI interaction**, tested with 49 design practitioners on 20 AI products ([Amershi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233)):
  - make clear what the system can do, and how well;
  - make it easy to dismiss and correct;
  - explain why it did what it did;
  - offer global controls.
- **IBM's Carbon design system** requires an AI label and an explanation popover on every AI output ([Carbon](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon-website/blob/main/src/pages/guidelines/carbon-for-ai/index.mdx)).
- **Think first, then see the AI's answer.** In an experiment with about 199 people, asking users to decide before seeing the AI's answer reduced over-reliance on it more than explanations did, though people liked it less ([Buçinca et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287)).

**What it should do:**
- **Ground every answer.** Base answers on the team's own runs, car versions and the rulebook, and cite them inline ("from Run 14, car v5").
- **Name one cause.** Give one ranked cause with a physical fix, as Track Titan and Coach Dave Delta do, and say so when nothing stands out.
- **Use real units with honest uncertainty.** For example: "Version 4 is probably faster, by about 0.012 s ± 0.009 s over 6 runs each."
- **Admit when data is thin.** State how much data a verdict needs ("needs 5 logged runs"). Individual runs last about a second, so differences of a few thousandths can be noise. That is the notes' inference; STEM Racing timing variation has not been measured.
- **Accept corrections.** One-tap corrections should update the team's records, not just the chat.
- **Show where text came from.** Label AI-drafted text for judged material as "Draft by AI, edited by Sam".
- **Offer a coach mode.** A teacher-switchable mode that asks for the team's hypothesis first.
- **Make answers shared items.** Post answers to the team, as Delta's Share & Compare does, rather than keeping them in private chats.

**What it should never do:**
- have a persona, remember emotions, or send "I missed you" follow-ups;
- offer unprompted congratulations;
- write the generic pep talk that sank Strava's and Garmin's AI launches.

**What the teacher needs:**
- an on/off switch for each team;
- safety flags visible to a designated adult, with students told what that adult can see;
- a published statement that student data never trains the model.

## Charge adults per team per season, and let sponsors pay

SubOne's model is a free team, with Pro bought only by an adult. It matches how youth-team software is sold (Heja, TeamSnap and GameChanger, above).

**The closest precedent is TrainingPeaks.** A coach pays for the platform and can pay for athletes' premium accounts: the Coach Edition costs $21.99 a month, and each extra premium athlete costs $9, falling to $4.50 each above 1,000 athletes. Athletes keep a free basic tier ([TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/pricing/for-coaches/)).

**Teachers expect competition software to be free.** Autodesk gives Fusion to "every student and teacher" in STEM Racing ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/education/competitions/stem-racing)), and Ansys provides free CFD software ([Ansys](https://www.ansys.com/academic/students/student-teams/stem-racing)). So a teacher's reference price for software for this competition is £0. Pro has to earn its fee through results the teacher can see: judge-ready packs, simulation and time saved.

**£69 a season is not an outlier.** The comparisons below convert at an assumed £1 = US$1.33 = €1.15, a round-number rate rather than a sourced one:
- **TeamSnap** charges per team, between about £53 and £75 a year for its first two tiers ([TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/teamsnap/pricing)).
- **Seesaw**'s list price of $11.95 per student comes to about £54 for a six-student team ([Vita-Learn](https://vita-learn.org/seesaw/)).
- **Hudl** charges high schools $1,500–4,000 per sport per year ([HoopBrief](https://hoopbrief.com/blog/how-much-does-hudl-cost-2026)).
- **Team budgets dwarf it.** One Portuguese team's regional-to-national season cost €2,155.71, so £69 is about 3.7% of it ([Caravel Racing](https://github.com/Caravel-Racing/CaravelRacing-f1/blob/3c5c12d179905556d9e5c70a08db93e88f3a27a1/server.js)). Irish teams estimated €40,000–50,000 for a World Finals trip, which puts £69 at about 0.2% ([Westmeath Independent](https://www.westmeathindependent.ie/2026/06/26/two-athlone-teams-seek-financial-backing-for-world-stem-racing-finals-in-singapore/)).

### Freemium alone will not fund SubOne

The conversion benchmarks are sobering:

| Source | Finding | Sample |
|---|---|---|
| Kyle Poyar, 2026 | Freemium: **3–5%** of accounts is "good", 8–12% "great". Only 7% of products run a "reverse trial" (a full-featured trial that drops to the free tier) | [Poyar](https://substack.com/@kylepoyar/note/c-209940962) |
| ChartMogul, 2026 | Median conversion 8%, but "very few products actually have an 8% conversion rate". One in five convert under 2.5% | 200 products, probably the same dataset as Poyar's ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)) |
| Lenny Rachitsky, 2023 | Freemium with some sales help converts roughly twice as well as self-serve freemium | Survey of 1,000+ products ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion)) |

These figures come from business software earning $50–249 a month per customer. SubOne earns £5.75 a month per paying team, so treat them as an upper reference, not a target.

**The revenue arithmetic is modest:**

| Conversion | Paid teams per 1,000 active | Revenue at £69 |
|---|---|---|
| 3–5% ("good") | 30–50 | £2,070–3,450 a year |
| 8–12% ("great") | 80–120 | £5,520–8,280 a year |

**The market is small.** The 2025 World Finals drew **83 teams from 34 countries** ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/news/lunar-crowned-aramco-stem-racing-world-champions-2025)), and teams have three to six students. No worldwide team count was found, but the base is plausibly thousands of teams, not millions.

Team-by-team upgrades therefore cannot be the main source of income. For SubOne, "sales help" means documents rather than salespeople: quotes, invoices ready for a purchase order (PO), a data-protection pack and a sponsor payment link. School, trust, sponsor and organiser deals are the main levers.

### The monthly price undercuts the season pass

At £9 a month, the £69 pass only pays off after **7.7 months**. Competition seasons are shorter than that:

| Team | Season | Cost at £9 a month | Compared with the £69 pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK | October to the late-March national finals (held 24–26 March in 2026) ([STEM Racing UK](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/stem-racing-uk-national-finals-2026)) | £54 | 22% cheaper |
| US | September to April | About £72 | About the same |
| World Finals | Runs to Singapore, 3–7 October 2026 | More than £69 | The only case where the pass clearly wins |

Seasonal churn is real. When Zwift raised its prices, users replied "I'll see you in the winter", meaning they would pay only in their season ([DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2024/05/increases-prices-hardware.html)).

Two changes fix this, though neither has been tested:
- **Make the pass run for 12 months from purchase** rather than to the end of a school year. That covers a World Finals in October and teams that buy mid-season.
- **Raise the monthly price to about £12**, so that six months (£72) costs more than the pass. At £12 a month, the pass pays off after 5.75 months.

TeamSnap's annual prices sit about 40% below twelve monthly payments, and SubOne's 36% discount is in line with that. The change is to present the pass as the main product and let monthly billing be the flexible exception.

Schools should also be able to:
- pay an invoice now and start later, so they can commit money inside their financial year;
- pause a team off-season without losing any data.

### Four plans, split by buyer rather than by feature

| Plan | Who buys | Price and unit | How they pay | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free | Nobody: the whole team uses it | £0 | None | Everything needed to finish a season, published as a "free forever" list |
| Pro season pass | Teacher, mentor, school or sponsor | £69 per team for 12 months (monthly option at about £12) | Card, invoice with PO, or sponsor link | Judge-ready packs, the full simulator, more car versions in 3D, a larger AI allowance |
| School or Trust | School, multi-academy trust or education board | Per team, cheaper at volume, as TrainingPeaks halves its per-athlete price (price not yet researched) | Invoice or PO, one data processing agreement | Several teams on one invoice, an admin view, the Schools pack |
| Organiser or region partner | National STEM Racing operator or corporate sponsor | Negotiated | Contract | Pro for every registered team in a region, paid from the partner's marketing budget |

The School tier follows how institutions already buy. Hudl sells whole athletic departments on one invoice, Heja offers club quotes, and Seesaw sells school licences.

The partner tier follows Autodesk and Ansys, who fund free software for every team. Given the small number of teams, this is the most plausible route to many paid teams.

Parent payment should stay out of the model. ClassDojo's parent-paid Plus works in a family setting, but UK advertising rules bar encouraging children to "make a nuisance of themselves" to adults, known as pester power ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/children-credulity.html)). A school that wants families to contribute can collect from them itself.

### Schools buy compliance first, and sponsors buy evidence

UK schools now treat software purchases as governance decisions. The Department for Education's guidance on buying education technology, published on **9 July 2026**, says: "When you buy or start using EdTech, you must make sure that the processing of personal data complies with data protection law" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/procuring-educational-technology-edtech)).

The ICO says its Children's Code applies to education technology unless the provider acts purely on the school's instructions, for the school's own purposes ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/the-children-s-code-and-education-technologies-edtech/)). Teams sign up to SubOne directly, and SubOne uses what it learns to improve its own product, so SubOne should assume the code applies to it directly. A £69 purchase is normally within a budget holder's authority, but only once the supplier passes these data-protection checks and can accept an official order.

SubOne therefore needs a downloadable **Schools pack** containing:
- a pre-filled data protection impact assessment (DPIA);
- a data processing agreement (DPA);
- a list of sub-processors and where data is stored;
- a retention and deletion schedule;
- an AI-use statement;
- the safeguarding controls from the previous section;
- a security summary;
- an accessibility statement.

Alongside the pack, SubOne should offer:
- invoices that carry the school's purchase order (PO) number;
- payment by bank transfer;
- a PDF quote a teacher can attach to a purchase request.

**Timing and other buyers:**
- **When UK schools spend.** Maintained schools' financial year runs April to March, and academies' September to August ([NGA](https://www.nga.org.uk/knowledge-centre/budget-setting-academy-trusts/)), so invoice campaigns belong in September and in February–March.
- **US districts** expect a signed student data privacy agreement. The national standard agreement's registry holds more than 130,000 signed agreements covering more than 12,000 schools and districts ([SDPC](https://privacy.a4l.org/national-dpa/)).
- **Teachers paying by card** are often using their own money. US teachers spent an average of **$895** of their own on classroom needs in 2024–25 ([AdoptAClassroom](https://www.adoptaclassroom.org/2025/06/09/2025-teacher-survey-spending-stats-classroom-needs/)). VAT receipts and a "send this invoice to my school's finance office" button therefore matter.

Sponsors are the most natural payers, because sponsorship is judged:
- The 2025 World Finals enterprise portfolio criteria include "Sponsorship & Return on Investment" ([World Finals regulations](https://www.scribd.com/document/878610006/STEM-Racing-World-Finals-Competition-Regulations-2025)).
- The 2026–27 rules make a Resource Manager responsible for "tracking expenses and income generated and allocating funds" ([2026–27 regulations](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d9375a869f15bc0bbdf98/1780323189128/STEM+Racing+Competition+Regulations+2026-2027+DEV+PRO+%281%29.pdf)).

A sponsor link should work like this:
1. The teacher sends the sponsor a checkout page or invoice made out to the sponsor company. It has fields for the company's VAT number and PO, and the sponsor needs no SubOne account.
2. Payment switches Pro on for the team.
3. The sponsor receives a VAT invoice and an optional "Software partner of Team X, 2026–27" certificate. The team can use it as return-on-investment evidence in its portfolio.
4. No sponsor branding appears to students unless the team chooses to add its own thanks, which keeps the promise of no ads.

This relies on payment links and on invoices that a third party can pay. Stripe is believed to offer both, but its documentation could not be checked.

### Upgrade prompts go to adults, at moments of need, and never to students

Several rules draw a strict line between students and money:
- **UK advertising code:** ads must not include "a direct exhortation to children to buy... or to persuade their parents, guardians or other persons to buy" ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/children-credulity.html)). That makes even "ask your teacher to get Pro" risky on a student's screen.
- **Apple:** apps in its Kids category may offer purchases only behind a parental gate ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
- **ICO:** forbids design nudges that steer children towards weaker privacy ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/13-nudge-techniques/)).
- **CMA and ICO:** the competition regulator and the ICO jointly name "confirmshaming" (guilt-tripping people who decline) and "biased framing" as harmful design ([CMA and ICO](https://www.drcf.org.uk/siteassets/drcf/pdf-files/harmful-design-in-digital-markets-ico-cma-joint-position-paper.pdf?v=380506)).
- **UK subscription rules:** from spring 2027, these are expected to require renewal reminders and two 14-day cooling-off periods ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/dmcc-subscription-rules-delayed-to-spring-2027-key-takeaways-from-the-consultation-response/)).
- **EU guidance:** anything shown to minors should be priced in national currency, with no virtual currencies ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/european-commission-issues-guidelines-on-the-protection-of-minors)).

The risk is real. A 2024 international review of 642 subscription websites and apps found that **76%** used at least one possible dark pattern ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-icpen-gpen-announce-results-review-use-dark-patterns-affecting-subscription-services-privacy)).

**What students see.** A student who reaches a Pro feature sees what it does and the line "Your team's plan is managed by your teacher or mentor". There is no button, price or countdown.

**What teachers see.** Teachers get factual prompts at moments of need:
- when a usage limit is reached, such as a fourth car version in 3D, an eleventh AI question in a month or a second judge pack;
- in the four to six weeks before a submission deadline;
- when students try a locked feature, reported as a team count and never by name.

"Not now" is as prominent as "Upgrade", and each prompt appears at most once per feature per week.

**Trials.** Once a season, SubOne can offer a 14-day Pro preview with no card required, timed to the team's first real deadline. It reverts to Free with nothing deleted, and never charges automatically.

**When Pro lapses, everything stays:**
- items over the free limits become read-only;
- judge packs already made stay downloadable;
- export stays free;
- a 14–30-day grace period follows a failed payment.

That is the opposite of Seesaw, whose free-plan users over its new limits "may need to delete content" after 30 June 2026 ([Seesaw](https://seesaw.com/pricing-packages/free-changes/)).

The fastest way to lose teachers is to take something back:
- Garmin's new paid tier drew an immediate backlash in March 2025 ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/live/live-garmin-connect-backlash-tell-us-what-you-think-about-garmins-new-premium-tier)).
- WHOOP reversed an upgrade fee within two days after members called it a broken promise ([Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/whoop-clarifies-updates-policy-after-member-pushback/)).
- TeamSnap forced users onto a new app that dropped features, and reviewers nicknamed it "TeamSnap Zero" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/teamsnap-one/id6670517733?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)).

SubOne should publish its "free forever" list, never move anything off it, and ship big changes between seasons.

### AI allowances need hard limits and season-length budgets

AI changes SubOne's costs. Practitioner sources put profit margins on AI products at about **50–60%**, against 80–90% for traditional software ([The SaaS CFO](https://www.thesaascfo.com/your-ai-feature-is-quietly-destroying-your-gross-margin/)).

A Pro team that used all 200 monthly questions would ask 2,400 a year. SubOne's actual cost per question is unknown, so these figures are only illustrative:

| Cost per question | Cost of a full Pro year | Share of the £69 fee |
|---|---|---|
| £0.005 | £12 | 17% |
| £0.01 | £24 | 35% |
| £0.03 | £72 | 104% |

Cost per question must therefore stay well under about £0.01, or the limits must change.

SubOne's allowances are in line with education norms. Notion's education plan includes only a 20-response AI trial ([Professional's Toolkit](https://professionalstoolkit.com/articles/notion-pricing)), and GitHub's student plan reportedly includes about 200 AI credits a month ([Fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/github-copilot-free-access/)).

The allowance should work like this:
- **Unit:** "race engineer questions per team", never credits or tokens.
- **Limits:** hard caps, no overage bills, and a plain count of questions remaining.
- **Budget period:** use will peak before deadlines, so a season allowance, or rollover within the season, fits better than a monthly reset.
- **Top-ups:** sold only to the adult buyer, in local currency.
- **Free teams:** still get the basic "one thing to work on", because paying for generic AI is exactly what users mocked at Garmin and Strava.

The pricing page should open with who pays: "Students never pay. A teacher or mentor can buy Pro by card, a school can pay by invoice or purchase order, and a sponsor can pay with a sponsor link." It should then:
- show Free, Pro and School side by side in local currency;
- state what happens when a pass ends;
- link to the Schools pack.

Widely repeated claims that three tiers and a "Most popular" badge lift conversion come without published methods ([Growigami](https://growigami.com/blog/saas-pricing-page-best-practices)), so any such badge should appear only if it is true.

SubOne should track paid teams in two ways: as a share of teams active this season, and as a share of teams that reach a milestone such as their first logged run.

Every research strand hit the same limits. A network proxy blocked most primary sites, and the shared search budget ran out. As a result, most findings were read through search-engine summaries or through word-for-word copies of primary documents hosted on GitHub.

**Most reliable: the design principles.** Two things make them sturdy:

**Least reliable: the numbers most likely to be quoted.** These are vendor benchmarks, practitioner claims about pricing, and some behavioural figures recalled from the literature rather than re-read.

## Build order: sixteen moves in three waves

Three lessons set the order:
- **Relearned navigation is expensive.** Discord's backlash shows that changing navigation people have already learned costs goodwill.
- **Mid-season removals drive teachers away.** TeamSnap's forced migration shows the damage.
- **School money moves at fixed times,** in September and in February–March.

So the frame and the trust essentials ship first, before the season's first deadlines. Everything shipped during the season only adds; nothing is taken away. Pricing structure and the larger bets wait for data from between seasons.

- **Engagement:** weekly active teams, and the share reaching activation within 14 days.
- **Notification health:** how often each notification is acted on, compared with how often it is muted.
- **Revenue:** paid teams as a share of teams active this season.
- **Usability:** a round of five-student usability tests for each age band before each wave ships.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "calm" means for SubOne. It is not a visual style; it is the business model seen from another side. Every rule that protects students also makes SubOne easier for an adult to approve, pay for or back:
- no streaks;
- no prices on student screens;
- digests before pings;
- no private adult–student chat;
- an AI that cites its sources.

These are what let a safeguarding lead approve SubOne, a teacher spend school money on it and a sponsor put its name to it. SubOne gives up some engagement by refusing Duolingo-style mechanics, but it wins that back when the buyer decides. The buyer is an adult whose trust depends on seeing that students are not being manipulated.

The same logic points to SubOne's most valuable growth feature: evidence that collects itself as the season runs. Judges assess project management and sponsorship, sponsors need proof of return, and teachers need to stop chasing students. A weekly check-in, an approval queue, a sponsor certificate and a season recap therefore do three jobs at once: they make the design calmer, they support students' learning, and they give adults a reason to pay.

The bigger risk now is not missing features but lost trust and creeping clutter. With a market of thousands of teams rather than millions, SubOne cannot afford a row like Strava's over its paywall or a forced migration like TeamSnap's. Linear's history shows that clutter returns whenever features grow. SubOne should therefore treat cutting back as a recurring job:
- a noise audit between seasons;
- a promise to only add features during a season, never take them away;
- success measured by teams making progress, not by time spent in the app.
