# Rulebooks and judges' points steer every season

A STEM Racing car (formerly F1 in Schools) is a CO2-powered miniature racer built within a tight kit and rulebook. Teams model the body in Autodesk Fusion around official envelope files, machine it in two halves from a 223 × 65 × 50 mm foam block on a school CNC router, print the wings and often the wheels, and fit a mandatory halo and helmet; they then sand, paint and weigh it to a class minimum before racing it 20 m in about 1.1 seconds at the top level. The car is only part of the contest: at the 2025 World Finals racing earned 250 of 1,000 points and engineering judging 340, while enterprise, presentation and project management earned 410. Of those, 220 points (team identity, pit display and verbal presentation) sit in work SubOne does not model today. Teams keep progress steady by planning backwards from hard submission dates that fall before the events (World Finals portfolios were due two weeks early, with a 20-point late penalty), by working in small owned tasks rather than long meetings, and by turning dated records into portfolio evidence. For SubOne the most urgent change is the spec check. Its 48 g example minimum matches no figure found in current rules: the top class is reported at 50 g or 60 g, and the Development minimum rises from 60 g to 80 g for 2026-27. The check should become a ruleset versioned by class and season that prices penalty points. Race logs should report averages and spread rather than best times, because a champion team measured a 20% error range in cartridge force output, and portfolio exports should enforce each format's page limits. One caution applies to everything below: the organiser's regulation PDFs could not be opened during the research, so every rule figure comes from search-engine extracts and must be checked against a team's own copy before SubOne encodes it.

## Foam body, mandated parts and a minimum mass that keeps moving

Every car starts as the same object. The official Model Block is a polyurethane-based foam blank measuring **223 × 65 × 50 mm**, with an **18 mm** pre-drilled hole for the 8 g CO2 "power pack". Denford lists it at 114 g and a reseller at 112 g, a 2 g gap no source resolves ([Denford](https://denford.co.uk/shop/stem-racing-model-block-blank-single); [WF Education](https://shop.wf-education.com/dnt/540-542.html); [Denford data sheet](https://denfordata.com/downloads/F1%20Model%20Block%20Technical%20Data%20Sheet%2029.6.15.pdf)). Cars for the 2026 World Finals must be cut from blocks bought from Denford or an official distributor ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/news/aramco-stem-racing-world-finals-2026-understanding-the-technical-regulations)). Around the body sit four wheels on two axles, two tether line guides that hold the car to the track's nylon line, front and rear wings, a mandatory standard halo and helmet and, in the Professional class, a "virtual cargo" volume the body must enclose. Race-grade cartridges are sold within **±0.5 g** of race weight ([Denford](https://denford.co.uk/shop/denford-power-packs-8gm-pack-of-360)). Their thrust varies far more, as the testing section shows.

The class decides how much of this a team designs itself. Entry teams use kit wheels, axles, bushes and screw-eye guides, and may hand-carve, print or machine the body ([Entry guidebook](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/692ec6999b44cc201847e7c6/1764673177837/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026+(1).pdf)). Development teams build everything from the starter kit except the wheels, which they may print ([Development regs](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)). For 2026-27 the Development class also allows CNC manufacture of the body and raises its minimum mass from 60.0 g to **80.0 g** ([Development regs 2026-27](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d66b2c057810a68895f00/1780311730822/STEM+Racing+Development+Technical+Regulations+2026-2027.pdf)). Professional and World Finals teams design most parts themselves, within limits such as a **65–85 mm** width and a **≤ 65 mm** height ([Professional regs 2025-26](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf)). The top-class minimum mass is contested. The UK uses the World Finals 2025 rules as its Professional regulations, and those are reported at **50.0 g**. Another extract gives **60.0 g** for the World Finals 2025 and 2026 rules, and a third mentions a 2025 "reduction in minimum car weight" without a new value ([Professional regs 2025-26](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf); [World Finals 2025 regs](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/sr_wf_2025_techregs_01042025.pdf); [World Finals 2025 regs, Brazil copy](https://stemracingbrazil.com.br/files/Regulamentos%20T%C3%A9cnicos%20Classe%20Profissional%20para%20Final%20Mundial%202025.pdf)).

The rules fall into three kinds: GENERAL ("shaping the car's fundamental look"), SAFETY and PERFORMANCE ([Development regs](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)). Specification judging starts each team at **100 points** (110 in some competitions) and inspects both race cars in a closed parc fermé using purpose-made gauges. Each infringement is deducted once per car. Proportional "PP+" penalties round any miss up to the next whole millimetre or gram and charge per unit ([Competition regs 2025-26](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6936b74056d414042f3a6e33/1765193536812/stem_racing_competition_regulations_2025-2026_dev_pro+(1).pdf); [World Finals 2025 regs](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)). Development charges 10 points for 0.1–1.0 g underweight and 30 for 2.1–3.0 g ([Development regs](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)). The Professional class charges 2.5 points per millimetre for each wheel outside its diameter or contact-width limit ([Professional regs](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf)). Rounding up and counting per car multiply small errors: a wheel profile 0.1 mm under the minimum costs 10 points per car across four wheels, and 20 if cars A and B share the error. Older rules also barred any car that broke a "critical regulation" from the national title, Fastest Car and Best Engineered Car ([2017 Professional regs](https://sangari.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/F1-Professional-Class-technical-regulations-2017.pdf)). Some checks run with a full 8 g cartridge fitted, and the car must then rest on all four wheels unaided ([Professional regs](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_professional_class_tech_regs_202526.pdf)).

The 2026 World Finals rules are "refinements rather than wholesale changes", aimed at areas that "previously caused confusion during scrutineering". They revise the safety tests for wheels, halo and tether guides. They also add an optional Pit Stop Challenge: a reusable wheel mounting, with no adhesives or permanent fixings, that lets a wheel be swapped in under **60 seconds** ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/news/aramco-stem-racing-world-finals-2026-understanding-the-technical-regulations)). The organiser tells teams to check compliance "during CAD, before manufacturing, and again before submission", three checkpoints that map directly onto software. One limitation affects all of these figures. Every research strand found the organiser's PDFs blocked and worked from search-engine extracts, which often left out clause numbers and sometimes mixed up classes. No extract gave the Professional length, wheelbase, track width, ground clearance, current wheel diameter and width limits, wing chord or virtual-cargo size.

## Fusion work starts from official envelopes and ends at a judge's laptop

Teams rarely start from a blank file. The organiser's downloads page offers a Fusion file built to the block's exact dimensions, class-specific CAD packs and a pre-configured Denford cutting-tool library that imports into Fusion. The Entry pack has six parts (block, body no-go-zone, halo, helmet, standard wheel and standard axle) and the Development pack five (block, no-go-zone, wheel, axle and cartridge) ([STEM Racing downloads](https://www.stemracing.com/downloads)). The Development no-go-zone is "a minimum safe working shape for the body". It comes with a 20 mm or 30 mm offset and the halo platform in one of four set positions, and the halo files come as .ipt, .stp, .f3d and .stl ([Development regs](https://www.scribd.com/document/967290440/STEM-Racing-Development-Technical-Regulations-2025-2026); [STEM Racing Canada downloads](https://www.stemracing.ca/downloads)). These choices fix parts of the body's envelope before any sculpting starts. The files also change every season (a new halo, the virtual cargo, the 80 g Development minimum), so reusing last year's file risks building to an out-of-date envelope.

Two modelling routes dominate. Autodesk's official tutorials run from beginner (customise a standard car) through intermediate (your own design) to advanced (design plus manufacture), all "sculpting from the model block" ([Autodesk Education](https://www.autodesk.com/education/competitions/stem-racing)). The beginner route edits T-spline bodies in Fusion's Form environment using Edit Form and a line of symmetry ([Autodesk tutorial copy](https://www.studocu.com/en-ca/document/port-credit-secondary-school/chemistry/fusion-360-tutorial-f1-car/29573307)). The route taught by a World Finals podium team is parametric. Alistair Horobin is design engineer of Nightingale, 3rd of 68 teams at the 2023 World Finals. His seven-part Off The Track series spends its second step on "Modelling the regulations" before the body is touched ([Off The Track](https://offthetrack.net/learn/); [Step 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b1jWOeqW2XI); [Autodesk Fusion blog](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/team-nightingale-f1-in-schools-world-finals-2023/)). Assemblies usually split into four components: wheels, main body, rear wing with its rear-wheel support, and front wing. They are "fully constrained using parameters wherever possible for upgradeable designs" ([Autodesk tutorial copy](https://www.studocu.com/en-ca/document/port-credit-secondary-school/chemistry/fusion-360-tutorial-f1-car/29573307)). Professional teams add their own wheels with bearing seats; one team sized its wheel bore at 7.2 mm to take 7 mm bearings ([Boca Bearings](https://www.bocabearings.com/blogs/2018/f1-in-schools-bearing-and-wheel-design)).

Compliance checking inside Fusion relies on the Inspect tools: Measure, Section Analysis, Interference, Center of Mass, zebra and curvature maps, and Accessibility and Minimum Radius analysis ([Autodesk Help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-Model/files/SLD-INSPECT-TOOLS.htm)). The Properties panel adds mass, volume and centre of gravity ([CAD Forum](https://www.cadforum.cz/en/how-to-find-volume-mass-or-surface-area-of-a-component-in-fusion-tip11320)). Predicting mass needs a custom material. Dividing the block's 112–114 g by its 724.75 cm³ gives about **0.155–0.157 g/cm³**, a derived figure that ignores the cartridge hole. The Entry guidebook warns that "gently sanding the model can reduce the size of the no-go-zone underneath minimum dimensions" ([Entry guidebook](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026.pdf)). That argues for designing a few tenths of a millimetre above every minimum.

Aerodynamic analysis happens outside Fusion. In 2019 Autodesk said Fusion "does not have any fluid dynamic simulation capabilities" ([Autodesk Community](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/fusion-support-forum/how-to-use-wind-tunnel-testing/td-p/8995114)), and no retrieved source shows that this has changed. Ansys is now the competition's "exclusive global partner for CFD simulation". It offers free Discovery licences, online courses and, since September 2025, a digital-twin racetrack for demo days at events ([Ansys](https://www.ansys.com/academic/students/student-teams/stem-racing); [Ansys course](https://innovationspace.ansys.com/product/cfd-simulation-of-a-stem-racing-model/); [Synopsys](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-09-29-Synopsys-Introduces-Digital-Twin-Racetrack-and-NVIDIA-Omniverse-to-STEM-Racing)). Schools also use Autodesk CFD, SimScale and Denford's Virtual Wind Tunnel ([Denford](https://denford.co.uk/products/virtual-wind-tunnel-software-2)). Most set-ups use a virtual tunnel with a **20 m/s** inlet, which is a realistic track speed ([SimScale](https://www.simscale.com/projects/Matheus_Alves/cfd_analysis_of_a_f1_in_schools_car/)). Exporting a faceted STL mesh instead of a solid produces errors such as "The facets are not well-connected" ([Autodesk CFD forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/cfd-forum/f1-in-schools-fusion-360-to-cfd-2023/td-p/11775658)). Teams improve one component at a time; a top team covered by Autodesk tested three designs of every component ([Autodesk Fusion blog](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/team-nightingale-f1-in-schools-world-finals-2023/)). A published case study cut drag by **9.89%** over rounds of CFD changes, checked in a physical tunnel ([JAEDS](https://jaeds.uitm.edu.my/index.php/jaeds/article/view/77)). Tachyon Racing found its Discovery results "closely matched" its wind-tunnel data ([Ansys case study](https://www.ansys.com/resource-center/case-study/stem-racing-competition)).

Design versions live in Fusion Team hubs, which provide member roles, version history and file recovery ([Fusion blog](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/how-to-use-fusion-team-to-collaborate-on-projects-with-anyone-anywhere/)). Autodesk's "save freely, version intentionally" change separates deliberate versions from routine saves ([Fusion blog](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/smarter-collaboration-autodesk-fusion/)). The CAD itself is then judged. Teams submit every part and assembly file named "teamnumber_team_name_country_filename", and must bring a laptop with the CAD software to demonstrate the design live at Design & Engineering judging ([World Finals 2025 regs](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)). A4 engineering drawings go into parc fermé with the car, alongside A4 renders ([Development regs](https://www.scribd.com/document/967290440/STEM-Racing-Development-Technical-Regulations-2025-2026)). The drawings must be "detailed such that they could theoretically be used to manufacture the fully assembled car by a third party", and third-angle projection is the norm ([Fusion drawing tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OUu3LSfsBeQ)). Fusion is free to every student and teacher through Autodesk's partnership, on education licences renewed each year ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/partners/autodesk); [Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/education/edu-software/fusion)). Teachers also get a free Fusion Fundamentals course ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/fusion-fundamentals)).

## Bodies are cut in two halves, finishing eats the mass margin, and single runs mislead

### Side-only machining pushes overhangs onto printed parts

Bodies are machined on school CNC routers, almost always Denford's, since Denford is the official equipment supplier. Teams use the F1 fixture on the F1 Router, Compact 1000 or Router 2600, or a 4th-axis rotary fixture ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/partners/denford); [REA guide](https://learning.rea.org.au/Guide/F1+Fixture+-+Side+Machining+-+QuickCAM+Pro+-+F1+in+Schools+Car+Tutorial/121)). Entry and Development cars may only be machined as a left side and a right side, so every machined surface must be visible from the side ([Yas in Schools](https://support.yasinschools.com/stem-racing/manufacturing/cnc-manufacturing-limitations/)). The right side is cut first, with the datum at the centre of the CO2 hole on the block's rear face. The block is then turned 180°, the mirror side is cut, and the axle holes are drilled by program ([Denford forum](https://denfordata.com/bb/viewtopic.php?f=61&t=6077&p=24737); [Entry guidebook](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/692ec6999b44cc201847e7c6/1764673177837/stem_racing_entry_competition_guidebook_2025_-_2026+(1).pdf)). Toolpaths come from one of two programs. Denford's QuickCAM Pro has 12 machining plans and a 6 mm ball-nose cutter by default; a smaller stepover gives a better finish at the cost of machine time ([QuickCAM datasheet](https://www.amtekcompany.com/pdf/datasheets/Denford/Denford_quickcam-pro.pdf); [REA guide](https://learning.rea.org.au/Guide/F1+Fixture+-+Side+Machining+-+QuickCAM+Pro+-+F1+in+Schools+Car+Tutorial/121)). The alternative is Fusion's Manufacture workspace with a Denford post-processor, which users say sometimes needs small hand edits ([Denford forum](https://www.denfordata.com/bb/viewtopic.php?t=5717)). Teams without a machine outsource the work. Denford sells a block that is shipped to UCL's MechSpace workshop for machining ([Denford](https://denford.co.uk/shop/stem-racing-model-block-blank-shipped-to-ucl-mechspace-for-manufacturing)). Roland DGA machined every body for SW1FT, winners of the 2025 Sponsorship & Marketing award ([Roland DGA](https://www.rolanddga.com/blog/team-sw1ft)). No source published machining times, feeds and speeds, or failure reports.

Because the body can only be cut from the sides, most other parts are printed. Budget teams print PLA wheels and struggle with seams and imbalance. Team Ignite's home-printed wheels lacked a smooth surface and had visible seams that left them unbalanced ([PCBWay](https://www.pcbway.com/project/sponsor/Team_Ignite_STEM_Racing_Car_Wheel_System_e649b6de.html)). Stronger teams move to resin, acetal, aluminium or "advanced engineering plastics" ([F1technical](https://www.f1technical.net/forum/viewtopic.php?f=14&t=21775); [Off The Track](https://offthetrack.net/news/hydron-in-their-own-words/)). One sponsored team reportedly printed hollow wheels so its rotating parts weighed under 1 g, and printed its rear wing as three parts for smoother surfaces ([CREATE Education](https://www.createeducation.com/blog/fm-1-team-racing-formula-1-schools-update/)). The 2026 wheel tests and the adhesive-free Pit Stop Challenge exist "to reduce wheel breakage", which confirms that glued-in, fragile wheels are a known failure ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/news/aramco-stem-racing-world-finals-2026-understanding-the-technical-regulations)).

### Paint is a mass budget that nobody has measured

Finishing runs from sanding and filing through putty and re-sanding to hand spraying, decals and varnish. Teams call paint "very heavy" ([Boca Bearings](https://www.bocabearings.com/f1-school-bearing-tips-tutorials/how-to-prepare-for-a-f1-in-schools-competition)), yet no source has measured how much mass it adds. The rules make that mass a budget. Ballast may only go in the container under the halo, and an underweight car has 0.2 g added for every 0.1 g it is short, on top of any Development penalty points ([World Finals 2026 regs](https://www.stemracing.com/s/SRWF26_Technical_Regulations-1.pdf); [Development regs](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)). Assembly centres on two things. A "sandwiching" hub design clamps each ceramic bearing to remove side-to-side play ([Boca Bearings](https://www.bocabearings.com/blogs/2018/f1-in-schools-bearing-and-wheel-design)). And the team builds two identical cars, A and B ([Competition regs](https://www.scribd.com/document/955986098/Stem-Racing-Competition-Regulations-2025-2026-Dev-Pro)). No public source explains how teams keep A and B matched.

### A 20% thrust scatter makes single runs meaningless

The standard test kit is Denford's race system: a 23.65 m elevated or 24 m roll-out track, a launch pod released by a solenoid, and infrared finish gates timing to 0.001 s. It has Auto, Manual and Reaction Time modes, sends race data over Wi-Fi, and offers optional sector gates whose split times produce acceleration and deceleration graphs ([Denford user guide](https://denfordata.com/downloads/F1RaceSystemUserManual(2101021).pdf); [Denford Race Control](https://denford.co.uk/products/stem-racing-race-system-package-stand-alone); [Denford sector gates](https://denford.co.uk/shop/sector-gate-timing-system)). Cars race 20 m head to head ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEM_Racing)). Reaction time runs from the moment the five start lights go out to the moment the driver presses the trigger. In reaction races it is subtracted from the total to give the car's race time ([Competition regs 2026-27](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d9375a869f15bc0bbdf98/1780323189128/STEM+Racing+Competition+Regulations+2026-2027+DEV+PRO+(1).pdf)). Lunar's 2025 world-title car is listed at 1.118 s for the Fastest Car award and is also credited with a 1.082 s run ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/news/lunar-crowned-aramco-stem-racing-world-champions-2025)). Either way it averages about 18 m/s over 20 m, consistent with the 20 m/s track speed that CFD set-ups assume. Since June 2025 there has also been an official benchtop wind tunnel, with smoke and airspeeds above 35 m/s ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/news/new-strategic-partnership-with-matrix-tsl)).

The most important testing lesson is statistical. Hydron, the 2022 world champions, measured a **20% error range** in the force output of CO2 cartridges. They concluded that timing prototypes on the track was "an inaccurate and inconsistent method of design evaluation" and built a 900-line Python race model instead. They reported a **15.61%** improvement from first prototype to final design ([Hydron portfolio](https://offthetrack.net/img/t41_hydron_australia_engineering_compressed.pdf); [Off The Track](https://offthetrack.net/news/hydron-in-their-own-words/)). Australia's getting-started guide has students calculate the average run time and standard deviation. Teams list weather, cartridge weight, car weight and track set-up as the variables to control ([REA](https://rea.org.au/wp-content/uploads/Getting-Started-V1.0.pdf); [Hydron portfolio](https://offthetrack.net/img/t41_hydron_australia_engineering_compressed.pdf)). No source offers a formal roll-down or spin-down friction protocol. Winning also takes several seasons. Hydron's title capped three years of work and six state and national events, and Lunar entered in 2022 and won in 2025 ([Off The Track](https://offthetrack.net/news/hydron-in-their-own-words/); [Lunar](https://www.lunaraus.com/story)).

## Enterprise work and the spoken pitch carry a third of World Finals points

An 80-strong panel drawn from STEM, motorsport, marketing, education and industry judged the 2025 World Finals ([Raceteq](https://www.raceteq.com/articles/2025/10/2025-aramco-stem-racing-world-finals-singapore)). Its 1,000 points split as follows, with enterprise (180) and the verbal presentation (140) together worth 32% ([World Finals 2025 regs](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf); [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/878610006/STEM-Racing-World-Finals-Competition-Regulations-2025)).

### Judges question every portfolio face to face

At World Finals level the enterprise portfolio is 12 A3 pages: a front cover, 10 content pages and a back cover, submitted as one digital and two printed copies ([World Finals 2025 regs](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)). Requirements vary by country and class. In the UK, Professional teams get up to 10 pages and Development teams 5 ([UK competition regs 2025-26](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_competition_regulations_2025-2026_dev_pro.pdf)). In the USA the Development class submits engineering and enterprise portfolios but no project-management portfolio, and Entry teams submit a simplified engineering portfolio and a tabletop pit display ([STEM Racing USA](https://www.stemracing.us/competition-classes)). Judges then test the story in person. Recoil Racing, the 2023 world champions, sat four interviews, one for each portfolio and one at their stand, and were "intensively questioned" about their marketing concept and business plan ([Recoil Racing](https://github.com/recoilRacing/-DM-HomepageFrontEnd/blob/e311d897ff61a5b149dad3260977d3b594552b4a/src/pages/BlogPage.tsx)).

### Sponsors buy visibility, but judges score mutual benefit

Team identity scores only 20 points directly, but it also shapes the pit display and the document-presentation criterion. The UK calls the area "Brand Identity", reportedly worth 100 points in a class the extract did not name ([UK National Finals](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/stem-racing-uk-national-finals-2026); [UK competition regs](https://f1inschools.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_competition_regulations_2025-2026_dev_pro.pdf)). Strong teams carry one theme through everything. Caravel Racing (Portugal) named its sponsor tiers after the crew of a caravel, a type of sailing ship ([Caravel Racing](https://github.com/Caravel-Racing/CaravelRacing-f1/blob/3c5c12d179905556d9e5c70a08db93e88f3a27a1/server.js)). Sailor (€0–150) buys a social-media mention. Helmsman (€150–400) adds a logo on the uniforms, and Master (€400–700) a logo on the car. Captain (€700+) gets prominent placement on car, uniform and stand, plus mentions in presentations. Orbit Racing (Ireland) charged €200 for Bronze and €500 for Silver, possibly in an earlier season ([Orbit Racing](https://github.com/DarraghON/DarraghON.github.io/blob/879dbe1903662567e65c591dfe7b15dba7d1a21d/pricing-section.txt)). The World Finals Sponsorship & Marketing Award, however, rewards "mutually beneficial relationships" alongside an effective marketing strategy ([Haas F1 Team](https://www.haasf1team.com/news/moneygram-haas-f1-team-supports-sponsorship-marketing-award-aramco-stem-racing-world-finals)). The best partnerships add engineering capacity. Roland DGA machined SW1FT's car bodies. A university workshop printed Recoil's front wings on a new resin printer, and Recoil hosted an eight-person delegation from its partner Schaeffler at its stand ([Roland DGA](https://www.rolanddga.com/blog/team-sw1ft); [Recoil Racing](https://github.com/recoilRacing/-DM-HomepageFrontEnd/blob/e311d897ff61a5b149dad3260977d3b594552b4a/src/pages/BlogPage.tsx)).

### Reach beyond existing followers is the metric worth showing

Digital media proficiency is a scored criterion ([Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/878610006/STEM-Racing-World-Finals-Competition-Regulations-2025)), and North America gives a separate Digital Media award ([SCCCA](https://www.sccca.org/news/2025/05/01/cultural-express/charles-wu-leads-team-sw1ft-take-3rd-at-stem-racing-north-america-finals/)). The most specific published measurements come from Caravel. A LEGO giveaway reached **3,072 views** and **634 interactions**, with **69.7%** of views from non-followers, and a bake sale drew **75.2%** of its views from non-followers ([Caravel Racing](https://github.com/Caravel-Racing/CaravelRacing-f1/blob/3c5c12d179905556d9e5c70a08db93e88f3a27a1/server.js)). The share of views from non-followers shows growth better than follower counts do. Sustainability evidence covers three areas: environmental (public transport, a stand built from BIOfoam), economic (reused pit-display materials) and social (visits to future school teams, donations) ([Caravel Racing](https://github.com/Caravel-Racing/CaravelRacing-f1/blob/3c5c12d179905556d9e5c70a08db93e88f3a27a1/server.js); [Recoil Racing](https://github.com/recoilRacing/-DM-HomepageFrontEnd/blob/e311d897ff61a5b149dad3260977d3b594552b4a/src/pages/SustainabilityPage.tsx)). No competition-specific rules on social media for under-18s surfaced. The USA operator's safeguarding policy covers social media, and UK youth-motorsport guidance says pages for riders under 18 should be "set up and monitored closely by the parent/guardian" ([STEM Racing USA](https://www.stemracing.us/child-youth-safeguarding-policy); [ACU](https://www.acu.org.uk/Uploaded/1/Documents/2025%20Paperwork/ACU-Social-Media-Policy-and-Guidance-2025.pdf)).

### The stand and the talk are judged like products

Organisers supply a booth frame and teams dress it. At the 2024 North American finals the frame was 94 inches tall and everything had to be hand-carried into the venue. Teams also had to provide a risk assessment and method statement for building the display ([Pit display supplement](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5d82b3ff7c2dda76efc9a139/t/65cd887dbad21171d110c248/1707968637506/PitDisplayInformation_SupplimentalDoc.pdf)). The verbal presentation runs 10 minutes at the World Finals (20 with an interpreter), from the team's own laptop ([World Finals 2025 regs](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/srwf_2025_competition_regulations_v1.pdf)). At 140 points it is worth 1.4 times the enterprise portfolio. Recoil rehearsed its talk in front of the Rotary Club Erlangen before winning Best Verbal Presentation in 2023 ([Recoil Racing](https://github.com/recoilRacing/-DM-HomepageFrontEnd/blob/e311d897ff61a5b149dad3260977d3b594552b4a/src/pages/BlogPage.tsx)).

### Qualifying turns a budget of thousands into tens of thousands

Caravel's campaign from regional to national final cost **€2,155.71**, including a **€1,555.06** marketing budget ([Caravel Racing](https://github.com/Caravel-Racing/CaravelRacing-f1/blob/3c5c12d179905556d9e5c70a08db93e88f3a27a1/server.js)). Irish teams qualifying for Singapore 2026 estimated **€40,000–50,000** to cover travel, accommodation, registration, backup cars, uniforms and a display screen ([Westmeath Independent](https://www.westmeathindependent.ie/2026/06/26/two-athlone-teams-seek-financial-backing-for-world-stem-racing-finals-in-singapore/); [Apex Racing GoFundMe](https://www.gofundme.com/f/apex-racing-world-finals-2026)). Team Successors documented its finances in two ways. It built a cash-flow forecast that deliberately erred on the cautious side and allowed for late sponsor payments. It also kept a shared log of every purchase with its value, date, category and receipt ([Team Successors](https://github.com/Exyywtf/Team-Successors-Portfolio/blob/0f38fc2c05c1971bd0cf9779d03db6ad5987cfb5/Portfolio/Qualifynals/project-management-plan.html)).

### Qualifying turns one season into two campaigns

A team that qualifies runs two campaigns: the national season, then a summer rebuild for the World Finals. That is roughly 12–14 months from a September start to the following October. Under the UK's 2026-27 rules, national finalists hand one of their two race cars to the organiser, so a World Finals campaign needs at least one new car. Deadlines act as design freezes, because the portfolios, drawings and CAD files must match the cars. The gap between those deadlines and the event varies. Recoil had to upload three portfolios translated into English, all its drawings and all its CAD data by 1 September 2023, ten days before the event began ([Recoil Racing](https://github.com/recoilRacing/-DM-HomepageFrontEnd/blob/e311d897ff61a5b149dad3260977d3b594552b4a/src/pages/BlogPage.tsx)); in 2025 the gap was fourteen days.

### Five standard roles often fall to three people

Teams have three to six students ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/aramco-stem-racing-world-finals)). The five standard roles are Team Manager, Design Engineer, Manufacturing Engineer, Graphic Designer and Resource Manager ([North America roles guide](https://f1inschoolsna.squarespace.com/s/Roles-on-an-F1-in-Schools-Team.pdf)). The 2026-27 rules make the Team Manager responsible for "project management of all key deliverables" and for getting the race cars ready. They make the Resource Manager responsible for "tracking expenses and income generated and allocating funds" ([Competition regs 2026-27](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6a1d9375a869f15bc0bbdf98/1780323189128/STEM+Racing+Competition+Regulations+2026-2027+DEV+PRO+(1).pdf)). With five roles and as few as three students, people double up. One Brazilian team built its own web app for this, covering member skills, a RACI matrix (who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed for each task), risk analysis and finance ([stemmanagement](https://github.com/PedroLutz/stemmanagement)). That suggests generic tools miss the combination this competition needs.

### A dated baseline for judges, a task board for the week

The organiser's Project Management Guide was written with the PMI Educational Foundation and follows PMBOK, the standard project-management reference. It covers planning, scheduling, risk, stakeholders, budgeting and continuous improvement. It defines the Gantt chart and names Word, Excel, Asana, JIRA, Microsoft Project, Miro, Monday, Smartsheet, Trello and projectmanager.com as tools ([Project Management Guide](https://www.stemracing.com/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_-_project_management_guide_2025.pdf)). Judges score against scorecards of key performance indicators ([STEM Racing](https://www.stemracing.com/partners/pmief)). The most widely read student critique, by former competitor Pannya Trehan, argues that teams draw Gantt charts "without knowing what each phase/step of the competition entails". He recommends breaking the work into a hierarchy of deliverables, then running lists of small tasks on a Kanban board ([Trehan](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/why-you-will-never-have-enough-time-in-f1-in-schools-and-gantt-charts-wont-help-65b58800d24d); [Trehan](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-use-work-breakdown-structure-f1-schools-pannya-trehan)). He calls whole-team meetings "shallow work" and prefers short, rare meetings plus focused individual work. He also describes burnout from "too many long nights, too many early mornings" as common ([Trehan](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/why-team-meeting-in-f1-in-schools-are-a-waste-of-time-how-to-truly-make-the-most-out-of-f1-in-f3a68cadaedf); [Trehan](https://medium.com/f1-in-schools-student-life/how-to-achieve-more-in-f1-in-schools-with-less-effort-time-why-you-should-be-careful-of-burn-f6e20fe23539)). The two views fit together: a dated Gantt baseline for the judges, and a task board for the week. Older UK guidance sets a floor of one hour a week through the first two half-terms ([Teachwire](https://www.teachwire.net/products/f1-in-schools-stem-programme/)). Finalists clearly do far more, but no source put a number on it.

### Five pages must prove a year of steady work

The project-management portfolio is short: at most 5 printed pages under the 2025-26 Development and Professional rules ([Competition regs 2025-26](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6913005a89f25e1a2f5bcef5/t/6936b74056d414042f3a6e33/1765193536812/stem_racing_competition_regulations_2025-2026_dev_pro+(1).pdf)). Judges stop reading an over-length portfolio at the page limit ([World Finals 2025 regs, Brazil copy](https://static.portaldaindustria.com.br/media/filer_public/8c/fd/8cfdb641-28a4-41a1-8cd5-2a46f6c4c052/regulamento_da_competicao.pdf); [North America regs](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5d82b3ff7c2dda76efc9a139/t/67721fa157763069f3a5d05a/1735532453635/F1iS-NACompetitionRegulations2024_25_REV2.pdf)). The densest proof of steady progress is therefore dated evidence. That means schedules comparing the plan with what actually happened, the status history of each risk, a budget ledger, and an iteration table of date, version, test and result, plus lessons learned. This list is inferred from the guide's emphasis on tracking progress, because the scorecard lines themselves were not retrieved. The failure modes with sources are burnout, planning before understanding the competition's phases, unproductive meetings, and late submissions. No usable source covered exam periods, booking machine time, handovers between year groups, late sponsor payments or lost files.

Team identity (20), the pit display (60) and the verbal presentation (140) have no home. At 220 points, those three outweigh the whole enterprise portfolio. ### The rules check needs versions, classes and penalty prices

The fix is structural. A ruleset should be a versioned record identified by class, season and national organiser. Each limit should carry its clause number and units and its category (general, safety or performance), plus a flag if it is a critical regulation. It also needs a penalty model: per started millimetre or gram, per car, per wheel where relevant, with caps such as Development's 30-point weight band. Finally, each limit needs a verification record of who checked it, against which document, and when. At least one national organiser allows its rules to be reproduced only by registered schools ([REA](https://rea.org.au/rules-regulations/)). SubOne should therefore let the teacher attach the team's own copy and confirm the values, and should ship example values clearly marked as unverified. The output should read like a scrutineer's sheet: a predicted score out of 100 for car A and for car B, the ballast needed, and warnings when a measurement sits within a set margin of a limit, because sanding removes material and paint adds mass. It should run at the three moments the organiser names: in CAD, before manufacture and before submission. Timing matters. Development teams starting 2026-27 now face an 80 g minimum, so a check still set at 48 g or 60 g is 20–32 g off.

### Car, workshop and race records should capture drift and spread

The build list should mirror the real parts list. That means adding bearings and bushes, axle guides or tubes, the nose (one assembly with the front wing in Development), wing and wheel supports, the ballast container under the halo, decals and spares. Development allows up to two nose-and-front-wing assemblies, two rear-wing assemblies and four front wheels as spares ([Development regs](https://www.stemracing.co.uk/uploads/1/1/8/9/118908723/stem_racing_development_technical_regulations_2025-2026.pdf)). Every part needs a source flag, because the class decides which sources are legal: kit and unmodified, kit and modified within limits, bought, team-made, or sponsor-made. Development bodies may be CNC-machined from 2026-27, which brings more teams to the workshop features this season. Stages should follow the real sequence: CAM, side one, flip, side two, drilling, sanding and filling, paint, decals, clear coat, assembly and weighing. Weighing after each finishing stage produces the paint-mass data that no public source has. It also shows early when cars A and B start to drift apart.

Car versions should link to a deliberate Fusion version and gain a frozen state. Deliverables and the A/B measurements can then be checked against the geometry that was submitted. Drag entries should record force in newtons at a stated speed, alongside Cd and the frontal area it was calculated with, because a Cd without its reference area cannot be compared. They should also record the CFD tool and set-up (inlet speed, moving ground, mesh) and any correlation with wind-tunnel data. A version's predicted mass can come from CAD volume times block density, plus the printed parts.

The 3D viewer should accept STEP, the format of the organiser's templates and of the hand-off to CFD, where STL breaks imports. It should read the overall length, width and height into the spec check, since the rules measure the extremities of the assembled car. And it should overlay the organiser's envelope file as a ghost, reproducing the podium team's habit of modelling the regulations first. Deliverable checklists should encode what judges require. That means A4 drawings in third-angle projection, with enough dimensions and materials for a third party to build the car, and the virtual cargo dimensioned and hatched. It also means A4 renders, CAD files named to the organiser's pattern, and a laptop ready for the live demonstration.

The workshop should log two-sided machining as linked runs, recording fixture, datum, tool, stepover, CAM program and post-processor edits. It should log print jobs with material, printer, orientation and outcome (roundness, balance, bearing fit). Outsourced machining, whether a university service or a sponsor's mill, should be a bookable resource with a lead time. The handbook can be seeded with the failure modes the research surfaced: broken or glued wheels, print seams causing imbalance, bearing-seat fits, STL-to-CFD import errors, post-processor quirks, and sanding below a minimum dimension. Nobody publishes machining or iteration times, so the team's own logged durations should feed the playbook's estimates.

Race records need the statistics Hydron used. With cartridge thrust scattering by about 20%, "best time" misleads. SubOne should show the average, standard deviation and number of runs for each car version, and flag whether a difference between versions is bigger than the noise. Every run should record cartridge mass, car mass, weather, track set-up, lane, and whether it was car A or B. CSV import should accept sector splits and reaction times once the export formats of common timing systems are confirmed. The race-time model should be calibrated from the team's own runs and show a range rather than a single number, and the what-if should never set mass below the selected class minimum. Start-light practice should mirror the five-light sequence and build a record of each driver's reaction times, since reaction racing is scored. Roll-down and spin tests have no public protocol, so SubOne's own protocols should be fixed, explicit, and linked to the wheel and bearing records. A pit-stop timer for the under-60-second wheel change is a small, direct addition.

### Sponsor return needs proof, and three judged elements need homes

Judges score sponsorship on return on investment, and the award rewards mutually beneficial relationships. So each sponsor obligation should link to whatever fulfils it: a post, a livery version carrying the logo, a pit-display panel, or a presentation slide. That turns the obligations list into the evidence the enterprise interview probes. In-kind support such as machining time, printing or tunnel access needs a value field; no team in the research reported one. The budget should gain three things. The first is a monthly cash-flow forecast with a "sponsor pays late" scenario, as Team Successors built. The second is spending categories for engineering, enterprise and team management. The third is a second-campaign template for international travel, where €40,000–50,000 is the reported scale. A whole national campaign can cost about €2,200, so a Pro subscription needs to arrive as a clean receipt that a sponsor can cover.

Marketing should link each objective to an audience, tactics and measured results. It should capture views, interactions and non-follower share for each post, and hold campaigns (bake sales, giveaways, school visits) and press coverage. A sustainability log with environmental, economic and social entries would serve a scored criterion that SubOne ignores today. Most users are minors and no competition rule governs their accounts, so a teacher approval step for public posts and photo-consent flags are sensible defaults.

The 220-point gap needs three additions. The first is a brand kit (logo, colours, fonts, templates) that drives PDF exports, the livery and the display panels, since consistency feeds team identity, the pit display and document presentation. The second is a pit-display project that works like a second build. It should hold parts, budget lines and dated design iterations, since the design process itself is scored through the enterprise portfolio. It should also hold the organiser's frame dimensions, a hand-carry packing list, a risk assessment and method statement, and an interactive element for the functionality criterion. The third is a presentation module. It should hold an outline covering every judged area, the hand-offs between speakers, and timed rehearsals against the 10-minute limit. It should also collect feedback from outside audiences and question banks for the four interviews.

### Plans, portfolios, roles and the AI should follow the judges' calendar

The playbook should plan backwards from a chain of dates rather than a single event: registration, car submission, document submission and the event itself, at each stage from regional to international. It needs a branch for qualifying that opens a second campaign with a car rebuild, and design-freeze milestones set before document deadlines. Each season should open with a task to fetch the new rules and envelope files and re-select the ruleset. The organiser's stated dates must drive all this, because the gap between document deadline and event moved from ten days in 2023 to fourteen in 2025. Gantt baselines should be saved as snapshots, so the comparison between plan and actual becomes evidence. The risk register should keep a dated history of each risk's status. Workload warnings should flag single points of failure (a role with no backup) and sustained late-night activity, the burnout pattern students describe. On the Today screen, "next move" can rank tasks by points at stake and how close the deadline is, since the points table makes both computable. The weekly check-in should replace meetings rather than add to them. A debrief prompt after each test day or event keeps lessons flowing into the handbook. Decisions from chat, pinned into a dated log, are cheap evidence that the project is being monitored and controlled.

The portfolio module should tag evidence against the published criteria. For enterprise that means the five criteria; for project management, the four process groups. For design and engineering it means the sequence of design, analysis, prototypes and testing, development, and finishing. The module should also know which portfolios each class requires. Exports must match the format (A3 or US tabloid), enforce page caps because judges stop at the limit, and count covers the way the rule does. They should also remind teams about the required digital and printed copies and the late penalty. The richest exports are the ones SubOne can build from its own records: the plan-versus-actual schedule, the risk history, the budget ledger and the iteration table. Teams presenting in a second language will also want translation support.

Default roles should mirror the five standard ones, each owning its modules. The Design Engineer owns the car and the Manufacturing Engineer the workshop. The Graphic Designer owns brand, livery and marketing, the Resource Manager owns money, and the Team Manager owns the plan and portfolio. One person should be able to hold several roles, with a RACI view that names a backup for each. Teachers need approval rights over spending and posting, and admin rights similar to a Fusion hub owner's. No rule on how much adults may contribute was found, so keeping authorship visible is the safe default. The AI race engineer should answer from the team's attached ruleset, cite clauses, and say when a value is unverified. It should run mock judging interviews based on the team's own evidence, and coach rather than write portfolio text. A Turnitin post on integrity in the competition points to originality checks on portfolios, though its content could not be read ([Turnitin](https://www.turnitin.com/blog/f1-in-schools-on-track-to-ensure-integrity)). At least one team has published its own AI "portfolio judge" ([STEM-Racing-Judge](https://github.com/Diegoplopo69/STEM-Racing-Judge)), which shows both that the demand exists and that others are building for it. Finally, a season rollover should archive the car history and carry forward the handbook, sponsors (with renewal tasks), roles and lessons, because champions build over two or three seasons.

## Conclusion

The research's blind spots are exactly the data SubOne already collects. No public source gives machining times, paint mass, the length of a design-make-test loop, prototype counts per season, roll-down protocols or the effect of exam periods. SubOne's machining runs, weighings, test runs and check-ins record precisely those numbers. With consent, pooled and anonymised, they could become the benchmarks that the playbook and mass forecasts currently guess at ("teams like yours add X g of paint"). No guide or competing tool has that knowledge.

The deeper shift is from logging to evidencing. The competition gives three times as many points to judged work as to racing, and nearly every judged artefact (portfolios, drawings, the pit display, interview answers) is a dated record of process. The product that wins is the one that turns everyday work into evidence tagged to the criteria automatically, and keeps that evidence true to one frozen car. The first move is the ruleset change. The 2026 international final runs 3–7 October, and new-season registration is open while rules have moved the Development minimum by 20 g. A versioned ruleset that the team supplies and verifies is the safer design. It also respects the limits some organisers place on reproducing their rules, and fits SubOne's policy of never naming the competition in its interface.
